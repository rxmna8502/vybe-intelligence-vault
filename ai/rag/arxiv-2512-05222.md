# Learning with Limited Antigenic Labels: Semi-supervised Prediction of Influenza A Antigenic Relationships with Protein Language Models

## Summary

arXiv:2512.05222v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Influenza A viruses (IAVs) undergo frequent antigenic changes that require regular vaccine updates. Because haemagglutination inhibition (HI) assays are labour-intensive, antigenic measurements remain limited relative to the available sequence data. We combined pre-trained protein representations with semi-supervised learning (SSL) to predict antigenic relationships when labelled data are scarce. HA1 sequences from H1N1, H3N2, H5N1, and H9N2 were encoded using ESM-2, ProtVec, ProtT5, and ProtBert. Using these embeddings, we compared self-training and label spreading with supervised baselines, including random forest (RF) and support vector machine (SVM) models. Nested cross-validation evaluated performance with 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100% of training labels retained. Self-training slightly improved RF performance in most settings but generally reduced SVM's ability to distinguish antigenically similar from antigenically different virus pairs. With 25% of training labels retained, self-training increased mean macro-F1 from 0.791 to 0.798 for ProtVec-RF and from 0.776 to 0.778 for ESM-2 RF. Label spreading performed best with ProtVec but remained below supervised RF across all protein representations and training-label proportions. In supervised and self-training RF models using ESM-2 or ProtBert, additional training labels improved classification of H3N2 antigenic relationships, whereas H9N2 remained the most difficult subtype. RF frequently misclassified antigenically similar H9N2 pairs as variants.
