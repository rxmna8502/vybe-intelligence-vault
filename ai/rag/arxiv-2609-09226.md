# Adaptive Entangled Neurons in the Brain and Natural Intelligence Simulations for Artificial General Intelligence

## Summary

arXiv:2609.09226v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Understanding the brain's mechanisms is crucial in neuroscience, particularly for simulating natural intelligence and advancing artificial general intelligence (AGI). Liu et al. (2024) and Deco et al. (2025) propose that quantum entanglement may underlie the synchronization of millions of neurons, introducing the Liu-Chen-Ao (LCA) hypothesis of entangled nerve fibers. However, directly testing this idea remains challenging. Here we employ a nonlocal many-body wave equation, introducing a generalized probability-wave framework to model collective behaviors among adaptive, interacting agents, particles, or neurons. This approach enables testable eigenmodes, offering an indirect means of examining the LCA hypothesis by observing patterns in collective trading behaviors. These patterns, rooted in behavioral psychology, are thought to mirror the internal brain's decision-making and underlying mechanisms. Our analysis of Chinese intraday stock market data shows that adaptive entangled game modes explain 82%-94% of decision patterns, sharply contrasting neoclassical finance predictions assuming independent rational agents. Additionally, 2-12% of actions adapt to news, events, and environmental changes, marked by rapid equilibrium shifts, while truly independent behaviors make up less than 5%. These results empirically support the LCA hypothesis and underscore the need to simulate the brain's mechanisms and generate adaptive, entangled modules for AGI. They also expose the shortcomings of conventional artificial neural networks (ANNs) that depend on vast, opaque parameters. By combining ANN-based AI with probability-wave-based entangled brain simulations, machine learning enables the creation of human-like processing units (HPUs). Such HPUs promise more efficient and robust AGI, particularly for embodied intelligence and robotics.
