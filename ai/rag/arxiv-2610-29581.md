# Unlocking Spatial Grounding in Flow-Matching Models via SNR Tuning

## Summary

arXiv:2610.29581v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Despite the success of foundation models in image synthesis, achieving fine-grained spatial control remains a significant challenge, typically requiring auxiliary structural adapters or complex inference-time steering. While modern vision-language (VLM) text encoders possess the latent capacity to represent spatial coordinates, generative models often fail to leverage this information. We demonstrate that the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) distribution is a critical determinant of spatial grounding in flow-matching models. Using a novel synthetic benchmark of geometric primitives, we isolate structural mechanics from textural complexity inherent in natural imagery and introduce a rigorous color-aware layout metric to quantify spatial fidelity. We show that training on a centered SNR distribution results in poor layout adherence (25.9%). Instead, we identify high-noise regimes as a "critical learning phase" for spatial grounding. Shifting the training focus toward these stages increases layout adherence to 80.8%, effectively reaching the 83.6% reconstruction ceiling of the underlying variational autoencoder. We find that the necessity for this shift scales with resolution and aspect ratio variance, and that the conservative noise schedules employed by models like FLUX.1 are insufficient for precise layout adherence. Finally, we show that these findings translate to real-world imagery via the COCO dataset (1.0 vs. 57.3 Average Precision), where state-of-the-art layout adherence is unlocked through distribution tuning alone, without architectural modifications.
