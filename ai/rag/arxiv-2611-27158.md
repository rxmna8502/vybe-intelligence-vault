# FM4SAR-Bench 1.0: Towards A Fair Benchmark for Evaluating Foundation Models for SAR Image Interpretation

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27158v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Foundation models have advanced SAR image interpretation but created a heterogeneous, fragmented model ecosystem. Existing models differ substantially in their input, training data, objectives, architectures, and release policy, yet the field lacks a unified taxonomy and comparative framework. Current evaluations rely primarily on task-specific benchmarks, which are ill-suited to fair comparison among models with fundamentally different input-output formats and task paradigms, and often overlook data transparency, reproducibility, interpretability, and deployment readiness. Limited SAR data accessibility also means high reported performance does not guarantee access to training data, code, or weights, limiting reproduction, benchmarking, and deployment. To address these issues, we propose a systematic taxonomy and hierarchical evaluation framework for SAR-related foundation models: five rating levels, six dimensions, and fine-grained criteria, with separate protocols for VFMs and VLMs. Beyond overall ratings, it yields interpretable multidimensional capability profiles and supports user-defined weighting, so rankings follow user priorities. Utilizing a fixed auxiliary multimodal large language model in combination with human review, we conduct a systematic meta-analysis and multidimensional assessment of 76 VFMs and 40 VLMs. In terms of SAR benchmark task performance under our framework, SAR-native VLMs do not significantly outperform remote sensing VLMs pretrained on mixed multimodal corpora in which SAR is one of several modalities. This lack of clear advantage is largely due to data availability: large-scale, high-quality, public annotated SAR data remain scarce, restricting both model and training-data scale for SAR-native models. The framework provides a structured basis for requirement-aware model selection and remains extensible as new models emerge.
