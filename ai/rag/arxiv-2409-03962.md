# Average Causal Effect Estimation in DAGs with Hidden Variables: Beyond Back-Door and Front-Door Criteria

## Summary

arXiv:2409.03962v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: The identification theory for causal effects in directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) with hidden variables is well established, but methods for estimation and inference beyond the g-formula remain less developed. Previous studies have introduced influence-function-based estimators for broad classes of identifiable effects. When implemented through the observed-data density factorization, these estimators can require conditional density estimation and numerical integration, which can be statistically challenging and computationally demanding for continuous or multivariate variables. Prior work on related causal functionals has used sequential regressions and Bayes-rule representations of density ratios to avoid these requirements. Building on established identification and influence-function results, we make these reparameterizations explicit for effects identified under treatment primal fixability, Tian and Pearl's necessary and sufficient condition for identification of post-intervention law, encompassing settings beyond classical back-door and front-door identification. We construct one-step estimators under alternative nuisance parameterizations and characterize how these choices affect robustness properties and nuisance-specific L2(P) product-rate conditions for asymptotic linearity and root-n consistency. The estimators accommodate data-adaptive machine learning for nuisance estimation. To address potential violations of the target parameter bounds by one-step estimators, we also develop targeted minimum loss-based estimators (TMLEs) that respect these bounds. Our robustness characterizations and sufficient rate conditions apply to both the one-step estimators and TMLEs. Building on prior efficiency theory for hidden-variable DAGs, we further develop modifications of our estimators that exploit ordinary independence restrictions. Implementations are provided in the R flexCausal package.
