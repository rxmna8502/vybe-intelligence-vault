# Learning Which Evidence-Seeking Decisions Matter: Calibrated Counterfactual Replay for Language Agents

## Summary

arXiv:2606.05263v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Language agents answer questions by searching, reading, and checking claims. A reward on the final answer says little about which of those decisions earned it, since an agent can reach the right string after redundant searches or with half the evidence missing. We treat the question as an experiment. At a saved decision point, PCCC-RL, for prefix-conditioned counterfactual credit, restores the tool state, runs the action the agent chose alongside a feasible alternative, and lets the same frozen policy finish both branches. The difference in final score then samples what the decision was worth. Replaying everything is too expensive, so the method predicts each action's credit up front, checks a random subset, and uses the recorded selection probabilities to correct the errors of that prediction, spending the budget where a further check can still move the training signal. Alternatives are drawn from the prefix alone, which keeps the comparison a usable gradient baseline. On HotpotQA and MuSiQue, joint exact match, requiring a correct answer together with complete supporting evidence, reaches 56.71% and 36.82%, gains of 4.19 and 3.86 points over a baseline with the same replay access. The gain concentrates in complete evidence chains and accumulates over many updates. Single-update effects stay uncertain, and citation coverage and scope distinctions such as "none" versus "not all" remain open.
