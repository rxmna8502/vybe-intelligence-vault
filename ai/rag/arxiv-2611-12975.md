# Attribute-Evidence Learning with Multimodal Prompt for Generalized Category Discovery

## Summary

arXiv:2611.12975v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Generalized category discovery (GCD) aims to discover novel categories from unlabeled data by leveraging limited labeled samples from old classes. Existing methods that rely solely on visual cues often neglect the multimodal nature of human perception, prompting recent approaches to incorporate textual information such as category tags and attributes. However, these approaches typically entangle global tags and local attributes within a shared global image representation, rendering attributes unreliable and potentially providing misleading cues that can drive the model toward incorrect predictions. To this end, we propose AptM-GCD, a novel multimodal framework that exploits apt textual guidance through attribute evidence learning and multimodal prompt reasoning. Specifically, we design an Attribute-Evidence Learning (AEL) mechanism that treats attributes as localized visual evidence rather than global category descriptors. By interacting attribute embeddings with patch-level visual tokens, AEL identifies reliable attribute-related regions and extracts discriminative local evidence, thereby reducing the impact of noisy or irrelevant attribute cues. Building upon the extracted evidence, we further develop a Multimodal Prompt Learning (MPL) mechanism to bridge local attribute semantics and global visual understanding. MPL converts fine-grained textual evidence into visual prompts and injects them into a multimodal prompt transformer, enabling attribute information to enhance category discrimination through contextual reasoning without enforcing direct global alignment between modalities. Extensive experiments on six datasets demonstrate that AptM-GCD consistently achieves SoTA performance across both fine-grained and generic benchmarks. Moreover, the proposed framework exhibits strong generalization ability, delivering consistent gains under unimodal and class-imbalanced GCD settings.
