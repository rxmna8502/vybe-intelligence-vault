# SCAR: State-Space Compression for AI-Based Radio Resource Management of Vehicular Services

## Summary

arXiv:2508.06243v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: The increasing demand for connected vehicular services poses significant challenges for AI-based network and service management due to the high volume and rapid variability of network state information. Traditional management and control mechanisms struggle to scale when processing fine-grained metrics such as Channel Quality Indicators (CQIs) in dynamic vehicular environments. To address this challenge, we propose SCAR (State-Space Compression for AI-Based Radio Resource Management), an edge-assisted framework that abstracts high-dimensional CQI information into compact representations for AI-based management. SCAR employs machine-learning (ML)-based compression techniques, including clustering and radial basis function (RBF) networks, to reduce the dimensionality of CQI-derived state information while preserving essential features relevant to management decisions. The resulting compressed states are used to train reinforcement learning (RL)-based management policies that dynamically adapt the throughput-fairness trade-off according to service-level fairness objectives defined by the NGMN. Simulation results show that CQI compression improves average NGMN feasible-region occupancy from 56.32% to 88.07% across ten RL algorithms, reaching 97.76% for the best-performing RL configuration, with limited throughput degradation and competitive computational overhead. Furthermore, simulated annealing with stochastic tunneling (SAST)-based clustering reduces state compression distortion by approximately 10% compared with conventional K-means clustering. These results demonstrate SCAR's ability to provide compact state representations for RL-based network and service management while improving service-level fairness in dynamic vehicular systems.
