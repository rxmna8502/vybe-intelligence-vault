# Alternating Abduction and Planning over Temporal Dynamic Knowledge Graphs

## Summary

arXiv:2607.27287v4 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Knowledge graphs change over time, but the effects of actions on them are only partly stated: the ontology entails further consequences, facts that an action needs are often unknown, and an action may make the graph inconsistent. We study these problems for Temporal Dynamic Knowledge Graphs (TDKGs), that is, SROIQ knowledge bases whose actions are rules with delete/insert templates and whose events lie on an intrinsic timeline. We present three tools: temporal projection, the exact test of plans; DDA (Direct-Derivation Abduction), which explains an observation by assuming its negation and negating what the reasoner newly derives; and APT (Abduction and Planning over TDKGs), which plans backwards from a goal, assumes the facts that the plan needs but nobody stated, and lets DDA explain the assumptions, each explanation becoming a sub-goal, up to a depth bound. The tools use a SROIQ reasoner only through a small contract, so they depend neither on a particular reasoner nor on a particular data structure; they are designed for a reasoner that processes added and removed axioms incrementally. Change is treated without modal operators, on one linear timeline and without possible worlds, so decidability of projection follows from that of SROIQ. We prove that indirect effects are exactly the entailments of the updated state, that DDA and APT are sound and terminate, and that bounded planning is 2NExpTime-hard and in 3ExpTime. We compare the tools with hitting-set abduction, the solver CATS and classical planning, and run them on six benchmarks and five reasoners with identical results.
