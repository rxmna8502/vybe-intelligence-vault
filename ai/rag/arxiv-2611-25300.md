# Federated Learning of Car Aerodynamics Surrogates with Spectral Optimizers

## Summary

arXiv:2611.25300v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Neural surrogates predict the aerodynamic forces on a car in seconds, compared with hours for a CFD simulation, but training them requires thousands of CFD simulations of different car designs, which car manufacturers keep proprietary. Federated learning allows several manufacturers to train a shared surrogate without exchanging data. We study which federated optimizer brings such a surrogate closest to one trained on pooled data. On DrivAerNet++, we train a Transolver surrogate across six clients that each hold cars of a single body type, and we compare three spectral optimizers, which orthogonalize the update of each weight matrix (FedMuon and two methods we propose, FedMARS-M and FedOLion), with three non-spectral ones (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD-Adam and FedLion), all tuned with the same protocol. FedMuon and FedMARS-M reach a drag error within 2% of centralized training with Muon, using two to four local epochs per round. The non-spectral methods perform best with sixteen local epochs, the largest number we tested, and still have a 17 to 37% higher drag error than FedMARS-M. We relate this gap to the low stable rank of the weight gradients, for which an orthogonalized step yields about twice the first-order decrease of a gradient step of the same spectral norm.
