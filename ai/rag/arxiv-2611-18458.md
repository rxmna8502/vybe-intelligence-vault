# When to Trust Simulated Patient Responses:Support-Gated World Models for Vasopressor Decision Support

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18458v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Clinical world models expose language-model agents to predicted patient responses, but simulations can be unreliable for poorly supported treatments or unfamiliar hospitals. We introduce Support-Gated World-Model decision support, a lightweight extension of a propose--simulate--refine agent. A recurrent dynamics ensemble predicts short-horizon physiological responses to documented norepinephrine dose categories, while a behavior model estimates observational action support. The interface withholds predictions with insufficient support or excessive ensemble disagreement and abstains when no candidate qualifies. Evaluation on eICU-CRD includes 70 hospitals and 30,700 decision transitions, with 14 hospitals held out for testing. At 62.0% factual-transition coverage, joint gating yields a MAP mean absolute error of 7.3 mmHg, compared with 9.2 mmHg for random selection at the same coverage. The gated agent achieves 60.0% agreement with recorded actions at 81.0% decision coverage, compared with 55.0% agreement and 99.5% coverage for an ungated ensemble agent. Component ablations show lower factual risk with joint gating than with either gate alone.These results characterize a reliability--coverage trade-off in selective clinical simulation; they do not identify individualized treatment effects or establish improved patient outcomes.
