# Deep Learning-Based Mesoscopic Emission Modeling

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33179v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Traffic-related emissions are a major contributor to urban air pollution. However, existing emission models face a fundamental trade-off: macroscopic models are computationally efficient but rely on average speeds, leading to systematic errors, while microscopic models achieve high accuracy but are computationally prohibitive for large scale simulations. To bridge this gap, this study introduces Direct Emission Prediction (DEP), a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)-based deep learning model. DEP was trained on over one million synthetic trips generated by coupling real-world GNSS speed profiles with the microscopic emission model MOBSim. By processing sequential link-level inputs, including vehicle characteristics, road attributes, and ambient temperature, DEP predicts CO$_2$ and NOx emissions. We integrated DEP into a MATSim-based workflow and applied it to the Ile-de-France region. DEP matches the accuracy of SPG-M, a hybrid mesoscopic-microscopic reference model, within 0.35% for CO$_2$ and 1.87% for NOx across both hot and cold-start scenarios, while reducing computational runtime to just 2.2%. Furthermore, validation against empirical remote sensing data confirms that DEP accurately reproduces real-world NOx emission levels across Euro 2-6 diesel vehicles, whereas traditional macroscopic tools like COPERT and HBEFA systematically underestimate them. Finally, DEP effectively captures the spatial distribution of cold-start emissions, identifying key hotspots in dense urban areas. This work demonstrates that deep learning-based mesoscopic frameworks can achieve microscopic accuracy at a fraction of the computational cost, enabling scalable, high-resolution urban emission modeling without relying on pre-computed emission factor databases.
