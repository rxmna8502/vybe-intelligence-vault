# Text-Informed Training Data Filtering for Enhanced Virtual Flow Meter Performance

## Summary

arXiv:2610.19860v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: In sectors such as energy, aquaculture and chemical processing, the effectiveness of both mechanistic and data-driven models is frequently limited by poor-quality training data, which obscures underlying physical laws and degrades predictive accuracy. Such quality issues commonly manifest as anomalies in the dataset, ranging from sensor faults to process upsets and incorrect operator inputs. While traditional rule-based filtering can remove a subset of the anomalies, it is unable to leverage contextual information stored as unstructured text, such as engineer comments and operational logs that explain under what conditions the data was collected. We propose a text-informed data filtering method that leverages large language models (LLMs) to integrate unstructured operational data with tabular sensor data. The method consists of two steps: (1) contextual anomaly detection by reasoning over both data modalities, and (2) generation of a binary mask that is applied to filter the training dataset for model development. To investigate the potential benefit, a steady-state soft-sensor regression model, namely a virtual flow meter that estimates well flow rates, is used as the downstream evaluation target. Experiments on a synthetic oil and gas dataset show improved model performance compared to traditional rule-based filtering. Ablation studies show that textual operator comments can provide filtering-relevant signals beyond sensor data alone, with the multimodal approach reducing downstream MAPE from 7.1% to 5.4% relative to sensor-only filtering. This work demonstrates that integrating unstructured operational knowledge with sensor data through LLMs can improve data quality for industrial machine learning applications, offering a scalable alternative to manual data curation.
