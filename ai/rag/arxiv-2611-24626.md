# Transferring an analysis of human brain organization to language models

## Summary

arXiv:2611.24626v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: During human cortical development, functional systems become increasingly differentiated. We asked whether improved reasoning in language models involves a comparable regional reorganization. Applying diffusion-map embedding, used to characterize cortical organization, to matched base and reasoning-trained models from three families, we found broadly preserved gradient profiles with localized changes within a contiguous layer span. Grafting weights from these spans into instruction-tuned hosts improved mathematical reasoning on average, with gains rising with region overlap, while retaining more previously solved problems than grafts elsewhere. Across training, the span formed gradually during the early rise in performance, and grafts from intermediate stages helped. Grafts shifted most hosts partly towards their donors' behaviour and sometimes improved on problems the donors failed; the same analysis located a transferable span in a medical model. Together, these findings identify a reasoning-related region whose weights improve reasoning with low interference in the host's existing mathematical competence.
