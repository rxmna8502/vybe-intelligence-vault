# Glyph: An Interpretability Engine for AI-Driven Scientific Discovery from Clinical Data

## Summary

arXiv:2611.08922v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Automated scientific discovery has the potential to advance understanding of disease mechanisms and accelerate identification of early disease markers. Manual data annotation is infeasible at population scale, motivating methods that automatically extract pre-defined phenotypes from multimodal and unstructured clinical data. Such pre-defined feature sets determine and limit which associations these approaches can discover. This limitation extends to agentic AI systems that use these tools to query population-scale data. In contrast, foundation models (FMs) trained on large volumes of clinical data can perform tasks beyond their training objectives. This suggests their latent embeddings may represent novel or unexplored features that could yield insights into disease mechanisms, yet such latent features remain difficult to interpret. To address this challenge, we introduce Glyph, an automated multistage framework that extracts features from multimodal and unstructured clinical data using FMs and sparse autoencoders, interprets them as coded clinical concepts, and evaluates their value for clinical scientific discovery. We applied Glyph to abdominal CT volumes, CT radiology reports and laboratory measurements from 41,628 patients across different institutions. We identified 397 predictive concepts supported by 4,463 Bonferroni-significant associations. Moreover, a majority of discovered associations were automatically reproducible in held-out cohorts across different sites. Glyph concepts provided complementary associations to literature-derived markers, leading to gains in predictive capability. More broadly, these findings suggest that foundation model features revealed through interpretability methods could expand clinical understanding of disease. We release Glyph at https://github.com/StanfordMIMI/Glyph.
