# Embodiment-Classifier Guidance: Transferring Frozen VLAs Across Robot Dynamics without Demonstrations

## Summary

arXiv:2610.22576v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Flow-based policies such as $\pi_0$ are trained on a fixed set of embodiments, and transferring them to new ones is an open problem. To adapt a frozen $\pi_0$ to a new embodiment without additional demonstrations or parameter updates, we train a lightweight embodiment classifier to distinguish successful action chunks on the source body from those on the target body, using a few calibration tasks. On transfer tasks that $\pi_0$ saw on its source body but the classifier was never trained on, we nudge each denoising step of $\pi_0$ with the classifier's gradient, which estimates the direction from the distribution of successful actions on the source body toward that on the target body. On the LIBERO simulation benchmark with four modified controllers, embodiment-classifier guidance raises $\pi_0$'s average success on the target bodies from 72% to 80% across 12 suite-controller pairs, with a 31% relative reduction in failures (11 of 12 gain; 5 significant after Holm correction). In simulation, guidance is data-efficient, needing few target-body rollouts per task, which makes it promising for sim-to-real transfer, where rollouts are costly. Guidance is mixed on weak wrists and harmful on a locked wrist, when the classifier lacks data or favors infeasible motions. Collecting more target-body data with guided $\pi_0$ can fill such gaps: on one weak-wrist arm, guided $\pi_0$ solved a task on which unguided $\pi_0$ had zero successes in the original data, and retraining the classifier on the guided successes raised success from 52.5% to 70.7%.
