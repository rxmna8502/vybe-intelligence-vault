# Schr\"odinger--F\"ollmer Actor--Critic: Diffusion Policy Improvement with Finite-Sample Analysis

## Summary

arXiv:2609.33239v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Diffusion policies represent multimodal action distributions, but a $Q$-weighted update does not specify how to sample from the resulting target distribution. We propose Schr\"odinger--F\"ollmer Actor--Critic (SFAC), an offline-to-online reinforcement learning (RL) method for Kullback--Leibler (KL)-regularized policy improvement through conditional diffusion. A minimax Bellman critic estimates the action-value function, which defines an exponentially tilted target policy. A Doob $h$-transform expresses this update as a correction to the reference diffusion drift. The target drift is a weighted conditional mean of Brownian-bridge labels. We fit it by weighted regression using endpoints from the current policy and pointwise action-value evaluations. In the small-update regime, the KL-regularized update follows the natural policy-gradient direction, and the Doob correction represents the same local change in the space of diffusion drifts. Under suitable conditions, we derive finite-sample bounds that separate the errors from critic estimation, weighted neural drift regression, and diffusion discretization through a reverse-KL actor bound. The average-performance bound depends explicitly on the comparison coefficients over the policy iterations. Synthetic experiments assess approximation to prescribed tilted targets and sensitivity to the diffusion-step count. Across six offline-to-online continuous-control tasks, the $Q$-guided bridge-regression implementation achieves higher performance than its offline initialization after online policy improvement.
