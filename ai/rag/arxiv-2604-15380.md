# Exascale Multi-Task Graph Foundation Models for Imbalanced, Multi-Fidelity Atomistic Data

## Summary

arXiv:2604.15380v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: We present an exascale workflow for atomistic graph foundation models using HydraGNN. Multi-task learning enables joint training across 16 heterogeneous first-principles datasets representing over 467 million unique structures (over 544 million cumulative branch records) and covering 85+ elements. A shared graph neural network backbone with dataset-specific prediction heads accommodates the strongly imbalanced, multi-source, multi-fidelity corpus, supported by scalable ADIOS2/DDStore data management. Composition-conditioned weighting combines source-specific predictions into a single deployable model. Six large-scale hyperparameter optimization campaigns, followed by resource-aware continual training on 2,048 Frontier nodes, identify PaiNN as the lead architecture under a fixed computational budget. We evaluate scalability across Frontier, Aurora, and Perlmutter and characterize transfer across twelve down-stream tasks, including comparisons with pretrained UMA and MACE models under different adaptation strategies. These results reveal task-dependent transfer benefits and highlight the importance of matching adaptation strategies to downstream applications. Finally, we assess inference optimizations and BF16/FP32/FP64 precision tradeoffs, achieving the screening of 1.1 billion atomistic structures in approximately 50 seconds on Frontier. Together, these results establish a scalable framework for multi-source atomistic foundation model training, evaluation, and high-throughput deployment, thereby allowing for a fast and reliable exploration of vast chemical design spaces that are otherwise inaccessible to first-principles methods
