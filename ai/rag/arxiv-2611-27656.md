# Beyond Sample Hardness: Adaptive Data Selection through Learning Dynamics

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27656v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Data selection has emerged as an effective approach to reducing the model training costs. However, most existing methods often rely heavily on sample hardness, which may overlook differences in sample learning dynamics and lead to suboptimal sample selection. In this paper, we propose LeDAS, an end-to-end Learning Dynamics-Aware Selection framework for dynamic data selection, which jointly characterizes sample importance through hardness, learning progress, and deterioration, enabling adaptive sample prioritization throughout training with marginal additional computational overhead. Specifically, we track per-sample loss dynamics using exponential moving averages (EMA) to obtain three complementary signals that capture current learning difficulty, ongoing learning improvements, and potential degradation. To accommodate evolving training dynamics, we introduce Adaptive Metric Fusion, a contextual bandit-based mechanism that dynamically adjusts the relative importance of these signals based on training feedback. Furthermore, a bounded staleness compensation mechanism accounts for historical selection patterns by increasing the selection priority of samples that have not been selected for extended periods. Extensive experiments across diverse vision and language tasks demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of LeDAS. Compared with state-of-the-art baselines, LeDAS consistently achieves superior performance, matching or surpassing full-dataset training at a 50\% selection ratio on ImageNet-1K and in LLM supervised fine-tuning. Moreover, its modular and task-agnostic design enables the integration of complementary selection signals, demonstrating strong robustness to noisy and corrupted datasets.
