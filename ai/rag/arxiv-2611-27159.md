# From Acting to Learning: Experience Selection toward Continually Learning Agents

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27159v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: We study how selection rules and coverage constraints affect what a 4B language agent learns from successful interactions. The comparison covers nine selectors, two fixed experience pools, eight initialization seeds, and two sampling seeds, producing 288 trained students. Each student is evaluated on 128 development task groups with the same executor. With equal global selection freedom, 256 distinct contents, and 768 training presentations, optimizer-aware gradient selection reaches 81.45\% and 73.19\% success in the two pools. It improves on global random selection by 8.25 and 5.96 percentage points, and on global high-NLL selection by 4.10 and 4.05 points. All eight initialization-level contrasts are positive against both baselines in each pool. Relaxing task/action quotas improves gradient selection but lowers random-selection performance: the benefit of selection freedom depends on the ranking rule. A separately frozen replication on 128 reserved task groups is complete; gradient selection gains +7.03 points over random and +5.03 over high NLL after averaging the two pools equally.
