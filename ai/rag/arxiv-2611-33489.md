# Data-free continuous-time physics-informed neural networks for vortex shedding: a consistent pressure across time windows

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33489v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Without data, physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in continuous time have so far failed to develop vortex shedding past a circular cylinder in a domain symmetric about the wake centerline: the training converges to the steady symmetric solution. We obtain shedding at Re=100 with a continuous-time PINN trained over successive time windows, without any reference data and without temporal discretization, the symmetry being broken by an analytical, divergence-free initial condition. The Strouhal number and the mean drag coefficient agree with an OpenFOAM solution computed on the same domain to within 0.5%. The pressure, however, is discontinuous at the junctions between windows, and the lift coefficient is a staircase. We relate this discontinuity to the differential-algebraic structure of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, of index 2 after spatial discretization: a consistent initial condition of each window must satisfy the constraint $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0$ for the velocity, and the hidden constraint $\nabla\cdot\partial_t\mathbf{v}=0$, with $\partial_t\mathbf{v}$ given by the momentum equation, then determines the pressure. Imposing the pressure alone makes the solution continuous but not consistent; our remedy imposes the pressure at the junctions and adds this hidden constraint to the loss. Over five shedding periods, the jumps of the lift coefficient become 20 times smaller.
