# The Value of Memory in Language Agents

## Summary

arXiv:2610.31303v4 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Language agents carry information from past experience into future tasks whose demands may be unknown or change over time. We develop a decision-theoretic account of the capability available in history, retained in storage and realized through use, together with the costs of recovery and revision. Drawing on comparison of statistical experiments, information acquisition and the value of computation, we express these values in units of avoided task loss. Recovery and revision define attainable risk-cost profiles and preservation across uses. A cost-aware simulation condition and five minimal counterexamples establish how information timing, access, computation, feedback and demand shape these comparisons; a pointer with zero answer mutual information can reduce the cost of exact recovery. Allocation and recovery criteria identify when gains pay for estimation, selection and evidence access. Complete-split studies on LoCoMo, LongMemEval-S and PersonaMem cover 3,075 questions and two readers. They show complementary value from saved state and recovered evidence, task-dependent performance orderings, and the role of reuse in amortizing construction costs. Multi-project workflows connect correction to preservation and validation costs. Together, the theory and studies support decisions about what to retain, which recovery options to preserve, and how to adapt memory as evidence and objectives evolve.
