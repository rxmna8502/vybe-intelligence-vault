# OVAL: Output-Aware Local Page Bases for KV Cache Compression

## Summary

arXiv:2610.06686v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Long-context decoding, whether over long reasoning traces, long videos, or robot episodes, is increasingly limited by the memory of the key-value (KV) cache. Page-retrieval sparse attention makes each decoding step cheaper by attending only to the top-scoring KV pages. However, it still stores every page exactly, and adds a small digest per page for scoring, so the cache ends up slightly \emph{larger} than the full cache. We propose OVAL, which stores each page as a pair of output-aware low-rank bases that act as both the retrieval index and the storage. A key basis scores the page and rebuilds its keys when the page is selected, and a PCA basis rebuilds its values. The exact pages can then be dropped, and nothing extra is added to the cache. The key idea is that the key basis should be output-aware. A low-rank error in the keys perturbs the attention output at first order, but perturbs the page mass that retrieval ranks only at second order in expectation. OVAL therefore builds the key basis from a mix of key geometry (key PCA) and the attention-output error, with a per-head mixing weight set in closed form. At the same attended budget, OVAL is best or tied-best in eight of nine long-reasoning settings. It matches FullKV on MATH500 with 66\% of its memory, is 1.25 points above the best baseline on AIME24 with 56\%, and is on par with FullKV on LongBench~v2. On \mbox{Video-MME} and LVBench, it is within 0.32 points of exact-page retrieval at the same read, while storing 53\% of the full cache instead of 103\%. Its savings also stack with quantization and token pruning: with 4-bit bases, it is within 0.25 points of FullKV at 41\% of its memory.
