# VISOR: A Vision-Language Selector for Multimodal Time-Series Forecasting

## Summary

arXiv:2611.31780v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Recent time-series foundation models (TSFMs) forecast a distribution over futures, and multimodal time-series (TS) forecasting further exploits other modalities to improve forecasting accuracy. However, existing works have two limitations: 1) TSFMs reduce the predictive distribution to its median or mean, flattening diverse futures into a calm line, and 2) multimodal TS forecasting models incorporate text into the forecaster through TS-language alignment, which is substantially weaker than vision-language alignment. To address these limitations, we propose the VIsion-language SelectOR (VISOR), a multimodal TS forecasting framework that selects one plausible future instead of averaging across candidates, leveraging the alignment capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs) to select the most suitable candidate based on textual information. VISOR consists of two components: 1) a text-free stochastic predictor that samples diverse candidate futures, and 2) a VLM selector that reads the candidates as a single chart image and selects one based on the text. To avoid overly calm forecasting paths, we train the selector with a shape-aware target that evaluates each candidate based on both point-wise error and path shape. Across three benchmarks covering ten datasets, VISOR achieves the best average ranking in both point and volatility errors compared with TSFMs and multimodal TS forecasting models. Code: https://github.com/seunghan96/VISOR.
