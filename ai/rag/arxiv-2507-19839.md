# Stabilizing the Modality Gap: Null-Space Projected Continual Learning for Vision-Language Models

## Summary

arXiv:2507.19839v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: The modality gap is intrinsic to multimodal alignment, where embeddings from different modalities remain partially separated in a shared representation space. In continual learning, CLIP-based vision-language models (VLMs) not only suffer catastrophic forgetting but also experience modality gap drift that disrupts the shared embedding geometry. Such drift degrades cross-modal alignment and generalization. We propose FPCD, an efficient framework combining Fast Null-space Projection (FNP) and Contrastive Distillation (CD). FNP accumulates compact Gram matrices of previous tasks' activations and projects gradients onto layer-adaptive approximate null spaces to reduce interference with learned representations. By replacing singular value decomposition with symmetric eigendecomposition, FNP accelerates projection-basis construction by a factor of 5-9. CD uses a small reference set to align the bidirectional image-to-text and text-to-image similarity distributions of the fine-tuned model with CLIP, thereby preserving pretrained cross-modal relations. On the 11-task MTIL benchmark, FPCD achieves 87.6% Last, 76.4% Average, and 65.2% Transfer. It also maintains more stable modality gaps and stronger zero-shot retrieval performance on COCO Captions and Flickr30k than competing methods. These results highlight the importance of controlling modality gap drift during continual adaptation of VLMs.
