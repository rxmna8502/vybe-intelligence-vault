# FlowersBench: A FluidFlower-Inspired Dataset for Benchmarking Carbon Storage Surrogates Across Geological Structures

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33335v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Machine learning surrogates are emerging as a computationally efficient tool for uncertainty quantification in geological carbon storage (GCS). Geological structure constitutes a major source of uncertainty; however, existing benchmark simulation datasets predominantly vary petrophysical and operational parameters within a fixed geological setting. Generalisation to unseen geological structures therefore remains largely unexplored. We introduce FlowersBench, a benchmark dataset of 5,000 two-dimensional, reservoir-scale CO$_2$ storage simulations across 244 structural geometries. The geometries are sampled from an in-house structural generator inspired by the FluidFlower experiments and Case B of the 11th Society of Petroleum Engineers Comparative Solution Project (SPE11B). Each simulation is run with Open Porous Media (OPM) Flow over a 50-year injection period and a 500-year post-injection monitoring period. Test geometries are withheld from training to assess generalisation to unseen structural geometries. Two additional subsets support evaluation under structural shift. The first extends the parameter ranges of the structural generator beyond those used for training, and the second introduces an unseen topology of multiple parallel faults. A baseline U-Net surrogate is trained to predict CO$_2$ gas saturation. In representative test cases, the surrogate recovers the main migration pathways, including lateral spreading beneath low-permeability layers and upward flow along fault damage zones, with errors concentrated near the plume boundary. Under both structural shifts, prediction error exceeds that on the held-out geometries, and the increase in relative error is greater for the unseen fault topology. FlowersBench is publicly available and provides a common basis for evaluating GCS surrogates across geological structures.
