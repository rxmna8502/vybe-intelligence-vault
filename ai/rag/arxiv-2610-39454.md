# Kairos: Fast and Reliable Symbolic Task Planning for Autonomous Robots

## Summary

arXiv:2610.39454v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Robots that plan while they act need a task planner that reads the models their designers write, answers within milliseconds, and says what each answer means. Models are written in the Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL), which has grown from classical planning to durative actions, numeric resources, time windows and continuous change, while planners cover one fragment each and differ in what their failures mean. The contribution in artificial intelligence is Kairos, a single planner, not a portfolio of existing planners, that covers the whole language and derives what each answer guarantees from the search that produced it. We prove when the exhaustion of a search shows that no plan exists, and give a relaxation of temporal tasks that proves unsolvability where temporal search cannot. For the compression of durative actions, we give a criterion on the values that actions read, prove that compression is sound, and show that it is not complete, so its failure is never reported as a proof. An independent validator checks every plan. The engineering application is on-line task planning for robots: Kairos is open source, packaged for the Robot Operating System, and the default planner of a widely used planning framework. On 1,756 public tasks from nine suites, Kairos solves more tasks than the strongest evaluated planner of each suite within 100 milliseconds, 1 second and 30 seconds; it proves ten of eleven unsolvable tasks, and handles each of the seventeen language features probed, where no other planner handles more than eight.
