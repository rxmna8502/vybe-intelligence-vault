# Embodied AI Fails the Mirror Test: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33010v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Can a simple mirror expose the limitations of today's robotic manipulation policies? Inspired by the mirror self-recognition test in animal cognition, we investigate whether robot policies can reliably manipulate physical objects in scenes containing their reflections. We introduce RoboDojo-Mirror, a benchmark built on RoboDojo that preserves task objectives, success criteria, and policy checkpoints while comparing a mirror-free baseline with single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions. Across five policies spanning Vision-Language-Action models, World Action Models, and agentic policies, every mirror condition reduces aggregate success rates: averaged over the three conditions, the drop ranges from 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points across policies, and it reaches 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Because a mirror is also a physical obstacle, we compare it against geometrically matched non-reflective panels; 50--88\% of mirror-specific failures occur without recorded robot--panel contact, so direct collisions alone do not explain the degradation. Trajectory and contact analyses instead reveal recurring behaviors consistent with phantom targeting of reflected objects and spatial mislocalization of real ones. Targeted diagnostics further characterize grasping near reflections, policy-dependent pushing progress, and physical-target interaction under main-camera occlusion. Real-robot experiments with $\pi_{0.5}$ and FastWAM also show lower overall success rates under the upright-mirror and mirrored-tabletop conditions. These findings reveal a shared vulnerability to reflective environments across diverse robot policies and motivate reflection-aware evaluation of embodied intelligence. Code is available at https://github.com/iPPLab/robodojo-mirror.
