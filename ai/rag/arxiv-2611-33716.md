# Flipping the Coins: Training Physical Simulators through Hard Switches

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33716v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Hybrid simulators embed learnable components such as neural networks in physical solvers and train them on reference trajectories. Many scientific solvers feature hard switches, such as a tripping power line, an igniting grid cell, or depolarizing brain tissue, where the loss exhibits discrete jumps across switch flips, leaving standard automatic differentiation to capture only its smooth part. Existing training paradigms face a fundamentally challenging trade-off, as gradient-based relaxations smooth the switches and introduce bias across jumps, whereas derivative-free methods treat the solver as a black box and discard path gradients of the continuous dynamics. Here we introduce EventFlip, which flips a near-threshold switch from a stored state, replays the original solver, and measures the loss difference between the two runs, in effect a finite difference across the switch. By perturbing every threshold with small Gaussian noise, we cast each switch as a stochastic binary decision and derive an unbiased gradient estimator that combines the continuous path gradient with a jump term constructed from these counterfactual rollouts. EventFlip-Rand optimizes this noise-averaged objective, whereas EventFlip-Hard operates on the deterministic hard-switch simulator used at deployment and provably targets the complete discontinuous loss. Under an identical forward evaluation budget, we benchmark EventFlip against twelve baselines across a power-grid cascade, a detonation wave, and cortical spreading depolarization, achieving the lowest error on every task and reducing the strongest baseline's error by factors of 1.5, 1.4, and 1.4, respectively.
