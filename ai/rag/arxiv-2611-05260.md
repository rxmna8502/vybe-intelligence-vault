# NovoCRF: Globally Normalized De Novo Peptide Sequencing on a Prefix-Mass Lattice

## Summary

arXiv:2611.05260v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: De novo peptide sequencing has moved from dynamic programming over hand-designed or generative scores to large transformers that learn the task end to end. We present NovoCRF, a conditional random field (CRF) whose states are prefix masses, so that its paths are exactly the peptides within $\pm$0.06 Da of the precursor mass. A small network scores each edge from physics-derived ion-match features and is trained with its scores globally normalized over all mass-feasible peptides. To our knowledge, NovoCRF is the first de novo method trained with exact normalization over all mass-feasible peptides. Our model has 3.6M parameters and trains in about 27 GPU-hours. Because it is small, NovoCRF can also adapt to each run at inference time: it fine-tunes part of its weights on the run's own confident answers, recalibrates the run's precursor and fragment masses, and uses retention time, predicted with a pretrained indexed-retention-time model, as further evidence. On nine held-out data-dependent acquisition datasets, NovoCRF has the highest peptide recall on five, and the transformer model InstaNovo on the other four. Where the spectrum determines the peptide, on labels with at most one peptide bond without a b or y ion, NovoCRF outperforms RNovA, $\pi$-PrimeNovo and CasanovoV5 on every dataset and InstaNovo on six of nine. The transformer models lead where many fragment ions are missing, and there their recall is up to 29 points higher on peptides of their own training data than on other peptides, while NovoCRF's recall differs least between seen and unseen peptides. NovoCRF also searches modifications it was never trained on at their exact mass: on a public spike-in of peptides with non-natural residues it finds 69.2% of the labeled spectra, against 64.4% for RNovA. With a GPU it sequences 203 spectra/s in its default two passes, and on the CPU alone it still sequences 67 spectra/s.
