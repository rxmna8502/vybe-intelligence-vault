# Augmented Inverse Hybrid Weighting: Robust Inference under Deterministic and Random Distribution Shifts

## Summary

arXiv:2608.00701v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Reweighting source samples to match a target covariate distribution is a standard response to distribution shift when generalizing evidence across populations. It is well suited to deterministic, learnable covariate discrepancies, but can fail when source--target differences extend beyond covariate shift or when estimation of the density-ratio weights is unstable. We introduce a new model where the discrepancy between two population laws comprises a deterministic covariate shift and a non-systematic component, modeled as random perturbation of the probability space. This separates systematic shifts, treated as bias and corrected by reweighting, from random perturbations, treated as distributional uncertainty and reduced by dataset pooling. Under pure random perturbations, this principle yields Augmented Inverse Distance Weighting, which combines regression augmentation with variance-optimal dataset-level pooling and attains asymptotic variance no larger than exact covariate balancing. When both components are present, we develop Augmented Inverse Hybrid Weighting, which interpolates between AIDW and standard augmented importance weighting. Both estimators tradeoff sampling and distributional uncertainty via a distributional distance quantifying the strength of random perturbations. We establish asymptotic normality of both estimators and provide tuning and diagnostic tools. On three real-world multi-site datasets exhibiting diverse distribution shifts, the proposed methods consistently reduce mean-squared error compared with weighting baselines, and improve empirical coverage when covariate-shift adjustment alone undercovers.
