# A Cherry-Picking Approach to Large Load Shaping for More Effective Carbon Reduction

## Summary

arXiv:2601.17990v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Shaping multi-megawatt flexible loads such as data centers alters generator dispatch on the electric grid, affecting system-wide CO2 emissions and wholesale electricity costs. However, evaluating prevalent load-shaping signals - such as grid-average carbon intensity, locational marginal price, renewable generation, or commercial marginal emission estimates - has remained difficult because full-scale market-clearing models and generator bid data are rarely accessible outside system operators. Here we conduct year-long causal dispatch counterfactuals using a calibrated, nearly 8,900-bus replica of ERCOT's day-ahead Security Constrained Unit Commitment and Security Constrained Economic Dispatch market-clearing process across renewable-rich and fossil-dominated zones. We find that while LMP-based shaping outperforms other static signals in annual grid CO2 reductions, no single signal performs best across all operating conditions. Motivated by these insights, we propose an interpretable daily "cherry-picking" strategy that selects among practicable day-ahead signals using observable grid net demand and nodal minimum LMP, with thresholds learnable directly from historical market data. Cherry-picking achieves 2-3 times greater annual grid CO2 savings than static LMP shaping without increasing wholesale electricity cost relative to flat operation. Extending this framework to coordinated spatial and temporal shifting across two grid nodes increases CO2 reductions by over 70% compared to temporal-only shifting. Finally, because shaping load against region-wide average carbon intensity can backfire and raise physical grid emissions in fossil-heavy zones, our findings highlight the need for Scope 2 carbon-accounting frameworks to incorporate locational, price-aware, and congestion-aware signals.
