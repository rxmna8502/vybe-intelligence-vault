# Sparse corruption in low-rank matrix inference: the PCA benchmark

## Summary

arXiv:2511.11927v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a standard tool for extracting a low-rank signal from noisy observations. It is known that when applying PCA to a rank-one signal corrupted by a dense, homogeneous noise, in the large matrix size limit, the BBP transition occurs, where the emergence of an outlying eigenvalue and the alignment of the corresponding eigenvector occur at the same critical signal strength. Here we study the case of sparse noise corruption. The noise matrix is modelled as the adjacency matrix of a weighted undirected graph with finite average connectivity. Using the replica method, we analytically compute the typical top eigenvalue, the top eigenvector component density, and the overlap with the signal, through recursive distributional equations solved by population dynamics. We identify two signal-strength transitions as functions of graph connectivity: $\theta_{\rm crit}$, marking signal recovery by the top eigenvector and generalising the BBP transition, and $\theta_{\rm b}$, where the signal-related eigenvalue detaches from the bulk. For noise with nonzero mean, these transitions need not coincide because of a structural sparse-graph outlier, leading to a discontinuous transition in the overlap with the top eigenvector. For a Random Regular degree distribution, we obtain exact closed-form expressions for both thresholds, the signal eigenvalue and the squared overlap, and show that the same formulas also predict the overlap of the signal with the second eigenvector in the intermediate regime $\theta_{\rm b}<\theta\leq \theta_{\rm crit}$. For a Poissonian degree distribution, an approximate closure of the recursions yields fast and accurate predictions in the recovery phase that agree with exact results from population dynamics. We also recover dense-noise results in the large-connectivity limit, and validate the theory by numerical diagonalisation of large matrices.
