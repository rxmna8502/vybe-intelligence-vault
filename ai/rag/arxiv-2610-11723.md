# MultiWorldBench: Do Independently Controlled Views Describe One Shared World?

## Summary

arXiv:2610.11723v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Multiplayer world models must ensure that independently controlled views remain consistent with a single shared and persistent world. We introduce MultiWorldBench, a diagnostic Minecraft benchmark comprising 495 case configurations across seven task suites and ten capabilities, including independent control, cross-view motion, shared-state synchronization, persistence, structural reasoning, concurrent interaction, and delayed revisit. Using Engine GT as a reference, we evaluate two multiplayer world models, Solaris (MP) and Gamma-World, together with a single-player generation variant, Solaris (SP). Among the generated systems, Gamma-World achieves the highest ten-capability average at 21.39, followed by Solaris (MP) at 20.88 and Solaris (SP) at 10.06, while Engine GT reaches 91.69. Gamma-World performs better on several control, shared-state, and revisit capabilities, whereas Solaris (MP) leads in cross-view motion and race-condition consistency. Nevertheless, all generated systems score at most 8.00 on state persistence and 1.33 on structural consistency, and none succeeds in spatial reasoning or building-identity preservation. Human pairwise evaluation yields the same overall system ranking and shows strong alignment with the automatic evaluation, with a mean dimension-level Spearman correlation of 0.95. These results show that current multiplayer world models still exhibit a substantial gap relative to Engine GT in maintaining a coherent shared world.
