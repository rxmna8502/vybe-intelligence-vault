# Sex Differences in the Detection of Parkinson's Disease from Speech

## Summary

arXiv:2611.24741v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Parkinson's disease (PD) is the second most common neurodegenerative disease in the world, and 70\% to 75\% of PD patients suffer from speech disorders at some stage. Since PD affects men more frequently than women, previous publications tend to rely on unbalanced datasets that potentially introduce sex biases into trained models. This study investigates sex differences in PD detection from speech using deep learning and traditional machine learning approaches. We evaluated speech-based PD detection using embeddings from three pre-trained speech models, combined with handcrafted acoustic features and different classifiers. Sex-specific experiments were conducted on balanced subsets of four sustained phonation datasets, controlling for sex, disease status, and, where possible, age. All recordings were obtained during a sustained phonation task. Results show that female speakers were more accurately classified than male speakers in the larger dataset. Sex-specific results show that when the models are trained on one sex, they fail to generalize to the other one. Our results highlight the importance of including sex as a variable in the development of PD detection systems.
