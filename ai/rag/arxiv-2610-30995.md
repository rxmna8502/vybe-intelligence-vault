# EEG-Captioner: Grounding EEG-to-Language Generation in Fundamental Signal Properties

## Summary

arXiv:2610.30995v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Electroencephalography (EEG)-to-language models offer a promising approach for translating complex neural signals into human-readable descriptions. However, generating plausible language does not necessarily demonstrate that a model has learned to interpret the underlying EEG signal. A direct way to assess such interpretation is to probe objectively measurable properties of the signal itself. We therefore construct an EEG-language dataset of 160,781 segments annotated with signal-level question answering (QA) pairs, where each question targets one of five property families and requires a quantitative comparison involving spectral, spatial, temporal, or lateral signal properties. Each segment is also paired with a feature-grounded EEG caption. We further propose a Dual-Domain EEG Encoder that jointly models temporal waveforms and spectral-spatial representations, and introduce EEG-Captioner, a unified EEG-to-language framework trained with both signal-level QA and EEG caption supervision. Experiments show that the proposed encoder achieves the highest overall macro-averaged balanced accuracy (Macro-BA) among the compared EEG encoders, while EEG-Captioner achieves a Macro-BA of 88.81% on signal-level QA and improves caption factual consistency from 82.06% to 83.46% over caption-only language adaptation, demonstrating the complementary benefits of signal-level QA and caption supervision.
