# Predicting protein structures at any folding stage from sequence

## Summary

arXiv:2611.04287v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Protein structure prediction has largely focused on native conformations, leaving the continuum of unfolded states, folding intermediates, and native structures difficult to access. We present FoldScape, a sequence-conditioned deep learning framework for generating protein conformational ensembles at specified folding fractions $Q$. Trained on approximately one million conformations from 10,000 protein sequences, FoldScape combines coarse-grained folding and all-atom mechanical-unfolding trajectories. It reconstructs three-dimensional $C_\alpha$ chains through hierarchical sliding-window regressions of inter-residue distances, chain-level assembly, and a rectified-flow decoder. By conditioning on folding progress, FoldScape generates conformations from unfolded states through intermediates to native-state structures without protein-specific molecular dynamics at inference. In matched-sample comparisons, FoldScape achieves median conformational-space coverage of 0.989, 0.722, and 0.877 along radius of gyration, fraction of native contacts $Q$, and secondary-structure fraction, respectively, versus 0.05, 0.05, and 0.10 for seed-varied AlphaFold 3 ensembles. Coverage advantages are consistent across the evaluated proteins and robust to binning choices. Reconstruction accuracy remains approximately stable across the evaluated stages of a folding trajectory, and stride-one crop-and-superpose inference extends predictions to chains several times longer than the single-pass limit. Analyses of mechanical-unfolding trajectories show residue-resolved pathways, reproducible extension-conditioned observables across pulling velocities, and geometrically coherent segments in partially unfolded chains. FoldScape extends sequence-based structure prediction beyond a single folded endpoint to controllable conformational-ensemble generation across protein folding.
