# Algorithmic Insurance

## Summary

arXiv:2106.00839v4 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: When artificial intelligence (AI) systems make errors in high-stakes domains such as medical diagnosis or autonomous driving, a single algorithmic flaw across varying contexts can generate heterogeneous losses that challenge traditional insurance assumptions. Algorithmic insurance, financial coverage for AI-induced damages, is an emerging market, yet insurers struggle to price these risks and AI developers lack frameworks linking system design to liability exposure. We connect a key operational choice in binary classification, how practitioners set decision thresholds, to tail risk exposure. Using conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) to capture extreme losses, we prove that established approaches such as accuracy maximization can significantly increase worst-case losses relative to tail risk optimization, with penalties growing at least quadratically as thresholds deviate from optimal. We propose a liability insurance contract that mandates risk-aware thresholds and characterize when it creates value for AI providers, extending the analysis to degrading model performance and human oversight. In a mammography case study, accuracy-maximizing thresholds carry up to four times the tail risk of CVaR-optimal thresholds. Insurance contracts create 7-8% gains for well-calibrated firms through risk transfer and regulatory capital relief, and up to 105% for poorly calibrated firms through mandatory recalibration, while partially aligned firms can lose under a strict mandate, calling for threshold flexibility in contract design. Unlike traditional insurance that merely transfers risk, algorithmic insurance can act as both a financial instrument and an operational governance mechanism, enabling risk transfer while improving AI safety.
