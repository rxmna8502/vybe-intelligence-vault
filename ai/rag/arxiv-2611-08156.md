# La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling

## Summary

arXiv:2611.08156v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on vision-language backbones, tying policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. While recent world-action models mostly scale the video backbone and its data, we argue that the choice of latent space is just as critical, and that the policy should be trained natively in that space. We introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; it predicts future features with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art average RoboTwin 2.0 success under both clean-only and full training, and state-of-the-art LIBERO-Plus success with corrected instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competitors; with full training, its success scales with model size up to 2.1B. Its S and B policies run two to five times faster than Fast-WAM and Faster-WAM while outperforming both on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0, and La-JEPA outperforms the closest JEPA-based methods, LeapBot-WA and JEPA-WAM, without their pretrained policy backbones. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to $\pi_{0.5}$. A design study shows that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction improves the policy even when skipped at inference. Tailoring the policy's design, including its latent space, to control can thus matter more than the knowledge transferred from a pretrained generative backbone, suggesting that robot foundation models may benefit from architectures designed for control rather than inherited from generation.
