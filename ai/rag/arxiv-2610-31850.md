# Rethinking Multi-View Fusion for Urban Representation Learning

## Summary

arXiv:2610.31850v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Urban region representation learning encodes heterogeneous urban signals, such as mobility flows, points of interest, and land-use patterns, into unified embeddings for downstream tasks, including check-in prediction, crime forecasting, and service demand estimation. Existing approaches rely on globally shared fusion and joint optimization, implicitly assuming uniform modality importance and naturally balanced multi-objective training. However, urban data is inherently heterogeneous. Our empirical analysis reveals two structural issues: (i) modality utility varies markedly across regions, causing static fusion to overemphasize signals from certain modalities; and (ii) heterogeneous objectives exhibit persistent gradient imbalance, causing representational bias and unstable training. We present PRISM, a self-adjusting framework for adaptive multi-view learning. PRISM adapts sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing to learn region-specific view allocations, while a Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID)-inspired regulation mechanism mitigates gradient conflicts during optimization. Experiments on three large-scale urban datasets show that PRISM achieves more competitive performance than strong multi-view baselines, while providing more stable optimization behavior and more balanced region representations.
