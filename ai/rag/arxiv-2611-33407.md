# An Adversarial Multi-Agent Pursuit Game for Locomotor Agility Pretraining

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33407v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Agile humanoid locomotion requires controllers that can adapt to rapidly changing motion demands while remaining stable and robust. Recent locomotion controllers are typically designed with either a dense reward that is carefully shaped to encourage steps and penalize falling, or by using a reference tracking reward which teaches the controller to follow predetermined motions. Once a behavior that maximizes the reward is discovered, the controller ceases to learn. While generally stable, reference tracking methods focus on motor control separately from motion generation. Self-play has been proven as an effective technique for superhuman performance on several multiagent games like Go, Starcraft, hide-and-seek, and capture-the-flag. We train neural networks to play the game of multiagent tag in a physics simulator to study if new motion generation and control strategies can emerge through sparse feedback and competitive self-play. We present MJTag, a task suite for role-conditioned locomotion during competitive pursuit-evasion. Our model is a role-conditioned policy for a low-level locomotion controller and optional planar velocity commands. MJTag's league-based play with sparse feedback enables a locomotion controller to adopt new strategies, including dodging, sprinting, and tackling.
