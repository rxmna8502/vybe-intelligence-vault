# SIEVES: Selective Prediction Generalizes through Visual Evidence Scoring

## Summary

arXiv:2604.25855v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: As the accuracy of Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) on traditional visual question answering benchmarks approaches saturation, reliable deployment requires satisfying low error tolerances in real-world out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. Selective prediction aims to improve coverage, i.e. the share of inputs the system answers, while adhering to a user-defined risk level. This is typically achieved by assigning a confidence score to each answer and abstaining on those that fall below a certain threshold. Existing selective prediction methods rely on text-only reasoning and learn to predict confidences simply based on whether the answer was correct or not. In contrast, we introduce SIEVES (Selective Prediction through Visual Evidence Scoring), which first requires the answering model to provide visual evidence supporting its answer, by returning crops of the image alongside their textual reasoning and answer. Second, a lightweight selector is trained to predict the quality of this evidence, assessing how well the provided crops localize the relevant visual elements and if the final answer is coherent with this evidence. We show that SIEVES improves coverage by up to six times on challenging OOD benchmarks (V* Bench, HR-Bench-8k, MME-RealWorld-Lite, VizWiz, and AdVQA), compared to non-grounding baselines. Beyond better generalization to OOD tasks, the design of the SIEVES selector enables transfer to proprietary reasoners without access to their weights or logits, such as o3 and Gemini-3-Pro, providing coverage boosts beyond those attributable to accuracy alone. We highlight that SIEVES generalizes across all tested OOD datasets and reasoner models, without benchmark- or reasoner-specific training or adaptation.
