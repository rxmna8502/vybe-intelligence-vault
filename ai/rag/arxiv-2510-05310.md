# RAG Makes Guardrails Brittle? Investigating Robustness of Guardrails under RAG-style Contexts

## Summary

arXiv:2510.05310v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: With the increasing adoption of large language models (LLMs), ensuring the safety of LLM systems has become a pressing concern. External LLM-based guardrail models have emerged as a popular solution to screen unsafe inputs and outputs, but they are themselves fine-tuned or prompt-engineered LLMs vulnerable to data distribution shifts. In this paper, taking Retrieval Augmentation Generation (RAG) as a case study, we investigate how robust LLM-based guardrails are against additional information embedded in the context. Through a systematic evaluation of 3 Llama Guards and 2 GPT-oss models, we find that inserting benign retrieved documents flips the judgments of input and output guardrails in around 11% and 8% of cases respectively, with the majority of these flips (72% and 64%) reversing previously correct decisions. We further analyze the effect of each component in the augmented context: retrieved documents, user query, and LLM response. Two tested mitigations reveal that the robustness gap is not easily closed. These results expose a context-robustness gap in current guardrails.
