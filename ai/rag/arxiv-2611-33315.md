# Large-scale Model Merging via Recovery-aware Task Weighting

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33315v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Model merging combines multiple task-specific expert models into a single model. However, merging preserves expert capabilities unevenly across tasks. This imbalance is particularly pronounced in large-scale settings, where some tasks can suffer severe capability losses even when average accuracy remains high. To address this imbalance, we introduce Recovery-aware Task Weighting (RaTW), a model merging framework that assigns larger weights to tasks whose expert capabilities are poorly retained by an initial merged model. We quantify these recovery opportunities as the fraction of a task's labeled validation samples that its expert predicts correctly but the initial merged model gets wrong, and use the resulting task weights to re-merge the experts. To evaluate merging at large scale, we construct CLIPMerge-100, a 100-task benchmark with task-specific experts on CLIP ViT-L/14 and ViT-B/32. Across eleven merging methods with 20, 50, and 100 tasks on two CLIP backbones, RaTW improves both mean accuracy and tail20 (the mean expert-normalized accuracy of the 20% worst-retained tasks) in all 33 ViT-L/14 settings and 32 of 33 ViT-B/32 settings. We further introduce ReForge++, which combines RaTW with iterative model refinement and outperforms all evaluated baselines, including a matched-budget control, in both mean accuracy and tail20 across all settings.
