# Staged Multi-Agent Training for Human-Exoskeleton Co-Adaptation: Simulation-Based Learning and Real-World Metabolic and Biomechanical Validation

## Summary

arXiv:2608.00715v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Learning-based controllers can enable exoskeleton assistance to be trained entirely in physics-based simulation, but achieving effective assistance on real users remains challenging because the human and device continuously adapt to one another. We investigate a Staged Multi-Agent Training (SMAT) approach that jointly trains a musculoskeletal human actor and a bilateral hip-exoskeleton actor (control policy) to learn coordinated assistance strategies while accounting for human-device co-adaptation. The resulting policy was transferred to a physical hip exoskeleton and evaluated in eight healthy adults across users and locomotion conditions without subject-specific retraining. Indirect calorimetry was used to compare no-exoskeleton, passive, and active conditions. Active assistance reduced net metabolic rate by 19.7% relative to the passive device (p < 0.001). The policy produced predominantly positive hip mechanical power across subjects, with a positive-power ratio of 0.984, and maintained effective assistance across walking speeds and terrains beyond the training conditions. These results demonstrate that simulation-based multi-agent co-adaptation can produce a single transferable exoskeleton policy that elicits meaningful physiological benefits in real users without subject-specific retraining, providing a framework for reducing the experimental burden of human-in-the-loop controller development.
