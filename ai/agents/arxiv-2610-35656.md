# Provably Efficient Q-learning for Robust Reinforcement Learning

## Summary

arXiv:2610.35656v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Standard reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms based on Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) are vulnerable to sim-to-real gaps. To address this issue, distributionally robust RL algorithms based on robust MDPs (RMDPs) have been proposed, for ensuring that agents can perform reliably to unpredictable conditions in real-world environments. Despite of the rich literature, \revision{an efficient model-free method is still lacking} for robust RL, particularly for Markovian data, which is more relevant in practical applications. In this work, we take a step towards settling this problem by proposing a sample efficient (asynchronous Q-learning) algorithm for robust RL. In particular, consider a $\gamma$-discounted infinite-horizon RMDP with state space $\mathcal{S}$ and action space $\mathcal{A}$, where the uncertainty set is specified by the total variation (TV) distance with uncertainty level $\sigma$. Assuming access to a single trajectory of Markovian samples induced by a behavior policy and the nominal transition kernel, the sample complexity of the proposed Q-learning algorithm --- to achieve an entrywise $\varepsilon$-approximation of the optimal Q-function --- is bounded by $$ \frac{1}{\mu_{\mathsf{min}}\varepsilon^2(1-\gamma)^3\max\{1-\gamma,\sigma\}} + \frac{t_{\mathsf{mix}}}{\mu_{\mathsf{min}}(1-\gamma)} $$ up to logarithmic factors. Here, $t_{\mathsf{mix}}$ and $\mu_{\mathsf{min}}$ denote the mixing time and the minimum state-action occupancy probability of the sample trajectory, respectively. This result is nearly minimax optimal, with an additional factor of $\frac{1}{1-\gamma}$. Moreover, it achieves a significant improvement over existing bounds.
