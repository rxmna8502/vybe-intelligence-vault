# FIRE-Drive: Feasibility-Informed Risk Evaluation for End-to-End Diffusion Planning

## Summary

arXiv:2611.22297v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Diffusion-based end-to-end autonomous driving models have shown strong potential in generating multimodal planning trajectories. However, their performance still depends heavily on trajectory initialization, mode evaluation, and supervision quality. Weakly constrained noisy initialization may introduce infeasible proposals and increase the burden of denoising. Moreover, selecting the optimal mode remains challenging when candidate trajectories are geometrically similar but differ in safety, interaction risk, and progress. To address these issues, we propose FIRE-Drive, a feasibility-informed risk evaluation framework for end-to-end autonomous driving. FIRE-Drive improves diffusion-based planning through dynamic feasible anchor initialization, candidate-specific bird's-eye-view risk evidence, and compact high-quality candidate supervision with lightweight refinement. This design aligns the initial support, risk-aware scoring, and multimodal supervision of trajectory modes under a unified planning objective. It enables the diffusion decoder to focus on refining plausible trajectories while the scorer distinguishes modes using local risk evidence. Experiments on NAVSIM v1 show that FIRE-Drive achieves a Predictive Driver Model Score of 91.5 with a ResNet34 backbone. Additional closed-loop evaluation on 220 Bench2Drive routes yields a Driving Score of 71.43 and a Success Rate of 42.73%. Component ablations and a scorer comparison under matched candidate inputs support the contributions of feasible initialization, auxiliary supervision, and risk-informed selection. Our code is available at https://github.com/Latte00/FIRE-drive.
