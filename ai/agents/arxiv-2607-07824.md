# CPM-MultiAgent: A CPM-Grounded Appraisal Framework for Simulating Dynamic Patient Emotional Evolution in Healthcare Dialogue

## Summary

arXiv:2607.07824v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) enable patient simulation for healthcare communication training, where learners practice responding to clinical information, concerns, and emotional reactions. However, existing patient simulation and affective dialogue systems often represent patient emotions as static attributes or focus on recognizing emotions and generating empathetic responses. They provide limited support for modeling how a simulated patient's emotional state evolves in response to a healthcare professional's communication. Changes in patient emotion constitute interactional feedback that learners must recognize and respond to across dialogue turns. We draw on the Component Process Model (CPM), a psychological theory that explains emotion as a dynamic process shaped by event appraisal. We propose CPM-MultiAgent, a CPM-grounded multi-agent framework for dynamic patient emotion simulation in healthcare communication training. The framework uses an emotion taxonomy informed by medical literature and expert input, and updates emotional states through healthcare-related trigger identification, structured CPM appraisal, and emotion-state integration, accounting for the patient's profile, clinical context, and prior emotional state. We evaluate the framework through student-led conversations and controlled trigger cases, combining nursing-expert assessments, LLM-judge comparisons, component ablations, backbone checks, and student feedback.
