# Branch Geometry and Finite-Radius Sensitivity of Hard-ReLU Training

## Summary

arXiv:2608.30960v4 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Two nearby initializations can activate the same ReLU unit one gradient descent (GD) step apart. When their separation is proportional to the GD step size $\eta$, that one step can determine which initialization gives the lower outer loss. We derive the integer crossing steps for piecewise-$C^2$ dynamics with finitely many separated transverse events. Euler error shifts the crossing clock, and rounding an earlier crossing to a GD step can shift later crossings. With a common positive margin from the rounding boundaries, the expansion predicts the trained state with uniform $O(\eta^2)$ error. In a strongly convex ReLU risk, the branch prediction converges on three fixed directions. The best single-vector predictor has positive limiting error. The recursion also gives a sufficient directional radius for preserving the training masks. In contractive affine regions, verifying those masks and bounding the finite remainder can certify candidate ordering. Scalar and coupled examples test the two rounding effects. Small-network experiments measure response accuracy, candidate selection, and how often multiple entries of the activation mask change in one GD update.
