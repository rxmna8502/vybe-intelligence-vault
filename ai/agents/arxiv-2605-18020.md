# Federated Learning by Utility-Constrained Stochastic Aggregation for Improving Rational Participation

## Summary

arXiv:2605.18020v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Federated Learning (FL) algorithms implicitly assume that clients passively comply with server-side orchestration, sharing local model updates upon the server's request. However, this overlooks an important aspect of real-world cross-silo environments: clients are often rational agents who may prioritize their own interests, such as local model performance, over that of the global model. In settings with significant statistical heterogeneity, rational clients may opt out of the federation if the perceived benefits of collaboration fail to meet their local utility thresholds. Such attrition degrades global model performance and can lead to the collapse of the federated training process. To avoid such a scenario, in this work, we introduce FedUCA (Federated Learning by Utility-Constrained Stochastic Aggregation for Improving Rational Participation), a non-monetary framework that establishes a non-payment channel through which clients communicate their willingness to participate via their utilities. The server acts as an optimizer, identifying aggregation parameters that satisfy clients' individual rationality (IR) constraints to sustain the global model performance. FedUCA achieves this through a stochastic aggregation mechanism over Dirichlet-sampled mixture strategies, which induces an implicit utility surplus that helps satisfy participation constraints. Supported by theory and extensive experiments, FedUCA's focus on participation feasibility improves client retention and global model performance.
