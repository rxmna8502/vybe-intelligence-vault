# Curriculum-Adapted Robust Reinforcement Learning for UAV Deconfliction in Adversarial Environments

## Summary

arXiv:2506.21129v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Autonomous unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) increasingly rely on reinforcement learning (RL) for navigation, yet global navigation satellite system (GNSS) spoofing attacks can induce out-of-distribution observation shifts that corrupt value estimation and degrade mission performance. Existing robust RL methods often lack generalization to unseen attacks. To address this, we propose a curriculum-guided adaptation framework that progressively exposes a robust policy to gradient-based adversarial observation perturbations of increasing intensity while aligning temporal-difference (TD) error distributions across curriculum stages. By preserving TD-error consistency rather than targeting specific attack models, the framework promotes resilience across attack conditions. We further derive a TD-space generalization certificate that bounds performance degradation when the TD-error distribution under a test-time attack remains sufficiently close to that of the final curriculum stage. We evaluate the framework in a UAV deconfliction environment with dynamic 3D obstacles under unseen fixed and dynamic GNSS spoofing attacks. Under fixed spoofing, the curriculum-adapted (CurAd) policy achieves approximately 99-100% mission success across evaluated traffic densities, compared with 20-24% for the best-performing robust baseline, while also outperforming the non-robust vanilla baseline. Under dynamic obstacle-luring spoofing, CurAd achieves the highest episodic rewards and reduces median mission completion steps by up to 22% relative to the best-performing robust RL baseline. These findings demonstrate that adversarial curriculum adaptation transfers resilience from synthetic perturbations to unseen GNSS spoofing attacks without attack-specific retraining, enhancing robust autonomous UAV navigation.
