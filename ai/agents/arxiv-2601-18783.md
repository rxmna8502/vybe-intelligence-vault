# Preference-Conditioned Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning for Tactical Decision Making in Heavy-Duty Vehicles

## Summary

arXiv:2601.18783v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: A central difficulty in applying reinforcement learning to real-world applications such as autonomous driving is that conventional scalar reward formulations obtained by aggregating multiple often competing objectives may obscure the structure of their trade-offs. These challenges are particularly relevant to decision-making for heavy-duty vehicles, which requires balancing safety, efficiency, and operational costs under demanding commercial requirements. We present a preference-conditioned multi-objective reinforcement learning framework that explicitly represents these objectives and utilizes Generalized Policy Improvement and Proximal Policy Optimization to learn policies capturing different trade-offs between them. The proposed method approximates the Pareto front across three conflicting objectives: safety, quantified in terms of collisions and successful completion; energy efficiency, quantified using energy cost; and time efficiency, quantified using driver cost. We evaluate the framework in a scalable highway traffic simulation environment tailored for tactical decision making in heavy-duty trucks. It yields smooth and interpretable Pareto fronts, and evaluations across traffic densities and against an analytical baseline show that the learned policies capture meaningful trade-offs. The preference-conditioned policy enables transitions between different driving behaviors without retraining, demonstrating the potential of multi-objective reinforcement learning for adaptive decision making in autonomous heavy-duty vehicles.
