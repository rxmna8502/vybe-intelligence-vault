# At the Limits of Computability: Steps Towards Autonomous AI Discovery on the Busy Beaver Frontier

## Summary

arXiv:2610.22603v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Recent breakthroughs in AI for mathematics---ranging from Olympiad medal performances to resolving long-standing open conjectures---have rapidly expanded what automated systems can achieve. Yet these successes primarily concern predefined, human-formulated problems, leaving open how AI systems can autonomously uncover mathematical structure and formulate new questions. To push beyond static challenges, we explore the Busy Beaver (BB) domain, an uncomputable frontier that is fundamentally open-ended. Because no algorithm can compute the Busy Beaver number $\mathrm{BB}(n)$ for every $n$, this domain provides a natural setting for sustained exploration through mathematical conjecture and proof. In this paper, we introduce a Gemini-based agentic pipeline that automatically discovers accelerations of Turing machines, expressed as compact mathematical rules summarizing many execution steps. Our system integrates pattern recognition, automated conjecturing, informal mathematical reasoning, and formal theorem proving in Lean. With this framework, our system autonomously discovered and formally verified novel Collatz-like accelerations for eleven machines in $\mathrm{BB}(6)$. Subject to community acceptance, we provisionally classify these machines as Cryptids, namely machines whose blank-tape executions admit exact, compact mathematical descriptions but whose halting behavior remains unresolved. These results advance the analysis of $\mathrm{BB}(6)$ holdouts on the Busy Beaver frontier and demonstrate the potential of autonomous AI systems for open-ended mathematical discovery in fundamental computer science.
