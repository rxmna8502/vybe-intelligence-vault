# Mechanism-Based Intelligence: Differentiable Incentives for Rational Coordination and Equilibrium Alignment in Multi-Agent Systems

## Summary

arXiv:2512.20688v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Autonomous multi-agent systems struggle with the Hayekian Information problem (using dispersed private knowledge) and the Hurwiczian Incentive problem (aligning local actions with global objectives). I introduce Mechanism-Based Intelligence (MBI), an architecture that treats intelligence as emergent from the coordination of specialized modules rather than a single monolithic model. At its core, the Differentiable Price Mechanism computes the exact gradient of the system loss as a price; subtracting its own marginal cost, each agent obtains the incentive signal
 $$\mathbf{G}_i = - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{global}}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_i},$$
 and the transfers take the Groves form. Each agent's best response minimizes the global loss given the actions of others, so the negative global loss is an exact potential for the resulting game, with a unique equilibrium at the global optimum. Gradient-based play converges to it under a step-size condition when effort is costless, and stops near it otherwise: a satisficing rule halts each agent's search when its marginal gain no longer exceeds its marginal computational cost. The equilibrium requires no knowledge of agents' private costs, so asymmetric information causes no welfare loss; simultaneous gradient dynamics require only a public bound on cost curvature. Each iteration scales linearly ($\mathcal{O}(N)$) with the number of agents, though for losses summed over agents the iteration count also grows with $N$. For known, differentiable losses, MBI avoids the combinatorial search of Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes. Under convexity and smoothness conditions, MBI aligns agent self-interest with the Planner's objective, offering an auditable approach to multi-agent coordination.
