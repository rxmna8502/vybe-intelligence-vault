# Pramana: A Composable, Domain-Specific Backend for Empirical Networking Research

## Summary

arXiv:2607.26352v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Empirical networking research often tests a hypothesis with data the study itself generates, so accelerating this research requires shortening the time from forming a hypothesis to generating that data. Consider whether a bulk BBR download fairly shares its bottleneck with a Google Meet call. Testing this requires configuring a realistic bottleneck link, running the BBR transfer and the Meet call together, and collecting the call's quality metrics. Today a researcher builds this setup from scratch for each new idea; this delay from idea to data is the ideation-to-data gap. AI agents already propose hypotheses faster than researchers can generate data to test them. Closing the gap needs a backend that generates the data for each new hypothesis on demand, without a new setup.
 We envision Pramana, a composable backend for networking research designed as a thin waist, with research intents above it and execution substrates below. At its core, the intent specification separates the data a study needs (the intent) from where that data is generated (the substrate) and how it is produced (the mechanism), so one specification can target any substrate. We build a corpus of 255 data-generation intents from 66 published papers and find that the intent specification expresses all of them, while no existing tool covers more than 11%. Our proof-of-concept implementation covers 27% of these intents. For CCAnalyzer, one of the 66 papers, the implementation regenerated the data for one trial of each network setting and congestion-control algorithm in the paper's main experiment. We lay out what implementing the full design requires and why it needs a community effort.
