# When Words and Bodies Disagree: Realizing Verbal and Nonverbal Mismatch

## Summary

arXiv:2608.22731v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Traditional nonverbal behavior generation systems for virtual agents typically take utterances as input and generate behaviors that match or illustrate the spoken words. However, human nonverbal behavior is shaped by more than speech content. It is influenced by speaker roles, interpersonal relationships, social context, and cognitive and emotional states of interactants. Therefore, the nonverbal channel carries information or emotions hidden in words. Modeling these complex verbal-nonverbal mismatches is critical for realistic virtual agents, particularly in training applications. We propose a taxonomy of mismatches, strengthening, mitigating, and leaking, and evaluate whether LLMs can select contextually appropriate mismatched behaviors. Through human-subject studies, we assess whether these context-driven behaviors, realized in a virtual human framework, produce the intended effects. Our results demonstrate that LLMs, when prompted with social context, successfully select targeted mismatched behaviors (e.g., sarcasm). Furthermore, the human-subject evaluation confirms that these nonverbal cues significantly shift observers' perceptions of the agent's attitude and emotional valence. Participants successfully distinguished between distinct mismatch functions, such as strengthening, mitigating, and leaking, perceiving clear directional shifts in social meaning compared to matching baselines.
