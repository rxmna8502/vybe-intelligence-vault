# AI Scientists for Building Virtual-Cell Models

## Summary

arXiv:2508.02276v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Autonomous agents, from virtual laboratories to biological data-analysis agents and co-scientists, have begun to turn scientific goals into hypotheses. Whether they can go further and convert real, noisy and complex biological data into executable model hypotheses and architectures that encode testable assumptions remains unclear. Predicting how cells respond to genetic and chemical perturbations is a natural test of this ability: it is central to decoding disease mechanisms and therapeutic discovery, yet it demands cross-disciplinary expertise rarely concentrated in a single research group. Here, we introduce CellForge, an organized team of autonomous agents for single-cell perturbation modeling. Given a dataset and a task, it surveys the literature, parses the data, identifies relevant baselines, and formulates a model hypothesis through critic-mediated discussion among agents specialized in data modeling, single-cell biology, and deep learning. It then writes the training code, runs the experiments, and returns a trained predictor including architecture, code, and validation results. The architecture itself is the hypothesis: CellForge produces it end-to-end, with no human specifying the design, loss, or training protocol. Across six perturbation datasets spanning transcriptomic, epigenomic, and proteomic contexts, CellForge yields models that are competitive with hand-crafted state-of-the-art results on established tasks and extends the same workflow to paired multi-omic readouts. Blinded human evaluators rate CellForge's research plans as more scientifically sound and innovative than those from single LLMs or deep-research systems. In summary, we show autonomous agents can complete the full hypothesis-to-validation cycle in single-cell biology, with an AI effectively building another AI, in a complex setting that currently requires a team of human experts.
