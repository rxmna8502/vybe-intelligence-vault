# OAK-Mind: Zero-Training Mental Health Change Inference from Passive Sensing via Knowledge-Grounded Multi-Agent LLMs

## Summary

arXiv:2611.22248v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Wearable and smartphone sensors capture behavioral signals associated with mental health, yet inferring mental health outcomes from these signals has traditionally relied on supervised models requiring labeled data and offering limited insight into their predictions. Large language models (LLMs) offer new opportunities to reason over behavioral evidence, but existing LLM-based approaches to passive sensing have largely continued to rely on task-specific fine-tuning or sensor-specific pretraining. In this paper, we introduce OAK-Mind, a zerO-training, multi-Agent, Knowledge-grounded LLM pipeline for inferring mental health changes from passive sensing data. The pipeline organizes sensing data into behavioral dimensions including sleep, physical activity, location mobility, and smartphone use, and orchestrates specialized agents grounded in mental health literature to interpret longitudinal behavioral changes. It synthesizes their assessments and applies questionnaire-based symptom rating to infer changes relative to an individual's baseline assessment with human-readable rationale grounded in observed behavioral patterns and domain knowledge. Across datasets and prediction tasks, while without task-specific training, OAK-Mind achieves comparable or better performance than the most predictive supervised baselines trained on additional labeled samples. It also demonstrate consistent improvement upon direct prompting across four tested LLM backbones. Ablation analyses support the benefits of combining behavioral interpretation with symptom rating, distributing analysis across domain-specific agents, and incorporating domain knowledge. Our findings highlight the potential of knowledge-grounded, multi-agent LLMs for training-free inference of mental health changes from passive sensing data.
