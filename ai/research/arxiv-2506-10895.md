# AIR: Rethinking Image-Text Offset Alignment in Multimodal Contrastive Representation Space

## Summary

arXiv:2506.10895v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Image-text offset alignment, which refers to the consistency between visual transformation directions and their corresponding textual transformation directions, has become an important relational property in multimodal contrastive representation spaces such as CLIP and SigLIP. This property plays a central role in text-driven visual transformation tasks, including generative model domain adaptation and text-guided object-centric image editing, where textual offsets are used to guide visual transformations. These methods fundamentally rely on the assumption that image and text offsets are well aligned, such that textual transformation directions can provide reliable guidance for corresponding visual transformations. However, the validity of this alignment assumption has not been systematically examined. In this work, we question this foundational assumption by conducting a comprehensive empirical analysis of image-text offset alignment in multimodal contrastive representation space. Our findings reveal not only noticeable offset misalignment but also a meaningful positive correlation between image-text offset misalignment and semantic concept distance across six large datasets and eight contrastive vision-language models. Based on this discovery, we propose Adaptation with Iterative Refinement (AIR), a method that iteratively refines text offsets through anchor sampling and our proposed concept description learning to mitigate the downstream effect of image-text offset misalignment and improve guidance accuracy. Comprehensive experiments on zero-shot generative model domain adaptation and text-guided object-centric image editing across 40 setups, including qualitative, quantitative, and user studies, consistently show that AIR enables state-of-the-art performance in these tasks.
