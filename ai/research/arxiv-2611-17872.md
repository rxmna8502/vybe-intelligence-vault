# Protection-Oriented Assessment of Machine Learning-Based Fault Detection in Power Systems

## Summary

arXiv:2611.17872v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Machine learning (ML)-based fault detectors are increasingly proposed for power system protection, yet aggregate metrics can obscure the consequences of false and missed trips. We present a protection-oriented assessment of three ML detectors and a sequence-current reference on PROTECT-90, comprising 9,022 simulated fault episodes on a 90 kV double-circuit line. The assessment combines five window conditions, causal window-end labelling, episode-grouped cross-validation, and operating-point selection under a fixed security budget. It reports security, dependability, detection timing, decision margins, and episode-level nuisance assertions. Gradient-boosted trees, a convolutional neural network, and logistic regression achieve AUC-ROC values above 0.999, yet at a security budget of $10^{-3}$ their developed-fault missed-trip rates range from 0.007% to 1.99%. Post-clearing false-trip rates of approximately 0.12-0.16% per window coexist with nuisance assertions in 11.3-19.1% of episodes. Among the analysed physical descriptors, missed trips are most consistently associated with higher fault resistance within the sampled range. An analytical current proxy suggests a fault-current gradient only partly reproduced by waveform-derived current (rank correlation 0.62). No single topology state or faulted phase consistently dominates within the sampled domain. Because PROTECT-90 contains fault episodes only, security is assessed on pre-fault and post-clearing windows rather than independent non-fault disturbances.
