# One Neural Operator for Re-planning Low-Thrust Cislunar Transfers after Thrust Outages, at the Original or a Later Arrival

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33188v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Safe-mode events causing thrust outages are common in missions with electric propulsion. To demonstrate mission feasibility in such an event, missed-thrust margin studies are performed where thousands of outage scenarios need an exact re-plan solved. We train a neural operator that maps a nominal trajectory with an outage profile to a re-planned trajectory and to the probability that the recovery exists, for 270 low-thrust near-rectilinear halo orbit (NRHO) to distant retrograde orbit (DRO) transfers, at an allowed slip of up to 10 days. On unseen transfers and zero slip, its median control error on the severe half of outages is 1.20% compared to 14.0% for the best cheap re-planner benchmark. With the arrival slip as an input, the error is 1.67% against 23.2%. It also predicts the recoverability of an outage for a given arrival slip with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.997, while the minimum recoverable slip prediction is optimistic by more than half a day on only 6 of the 84 checked outages needing slip. At the original arrival, the ensemble spread of an operator variant with solver inputs has a rank correlation of 0.91 with the actual error, and performing exact solves on the 31% most uncertain severe outages reduces the 90th-percentile error from 8.5% to 2.3%. A query takes milliseconds without a solver, making this a useful tool for large outage studies where the cost of thousands of exact solves outweighs the time to train the operator.
