# Contrastive local learning in large nonlinear analog circuits

## Summary

arXiv:2611.17561v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Machine learning in physical hardware promises to reduce training cost by colocating computation, memory and adaptation within device physics. Contrastive local learning networks (CLLNs) offer a compelling route: an electrical circuit relaxes to equilibrium in free and weakly clamped states, and each adaptive element updates from local voltage contrasts rather than backpropagated error signals. Yet whether nonlinear transistor implementations can scale beyond proof-of-concept circuits, and what design principles control that scaling, have remained unknown. Here we show that nonlinear analog circuits trained by contrastive local rules can learn larger machine-learning tasks and remain robust under hardware nonidealities. Using device-level circuit simulations, we systematically explore initialization, operating regime, loss-dependent clamping and training parameters, and scale CLLNs beyond ten thousand adaptive edges. The same local rule trains classifiers that reach the accuracy regime of conventional digital methods on Ionosphere and handwritten-digit benchmarks, and a network that autoregressively generates language over a 122-token vocabulary. These results establish the feasibility of local learning in large nonlinear analog circuits.
