# AI Lifecycle-Aware Feasibility Framework for Split-RIC Orchestration in NTN O-RAN

## Summary

arXiv:2603.23252v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Integrating Artificial Intelligence (AI) into Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN) is constrained by the joint limits of satellite Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP), feeder-link capacity, and orbital connectivity, which directly affect O-RAN closed-loop control and AI lifecycle management. This paper studies the feasibility of distributing the O-RAN intelligence across Ground, LEO, and GEO segments through three representative deployments: (i) Ground-Centric control with telemetry streaming, (ii) a ground-LEO Split-RIC with on-board inference and store-and-forward model refresh, and (iii) GEO-LEO multi-layer control enabled by inter-satellite links. For each deployment, we derive closed-form expressions for lifecycle energy, near-real-time control latency, and model-refresh latency, while explicitly enforcing power-feasible spaceborne computational throughput. Pairwise analytical boundaries and numerical sensitivity analysis over telemetry volume, feeder-link conditions, inference workload, model longevity, and orbital waiting time identify the operating regions in which different placements are energetically preferable or satisfy model-refresh requirements. The results show that the architecture choice is inherently lifecycle- and constraint-dependent, providing decision-enabling primitives for Service Management and Orchestration (SMO) level AI placement and lifecycle orchestration in NTN O-RAN.
