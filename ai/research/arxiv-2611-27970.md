# On the Complete Feature Set for ADHD Classification in Children and Adolescents Based on EEG Signals

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27970v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder is a neurodevelopmental condition that can affect attention, activity and impulse control. Electroencephalography (EEG) records brain activity as children and adolescents perform cognitive tasks. However, comparing features derived from these recordings requires consistent signal preparation, shared analysis windows and documented quality decisions. We curated 669 existing BALLADEER recordings from 156 participants across five task conditions so that researchers can compare seven EEG feature families on the same time intervals for each participant and task. The dataset links continuous signals to precomputed measurements from 115 feature definitions or templates and records the quality decisions made for each recording. Analyses must still account for repeated measurements within participants, residual artefacts and differences between acquisition systems. Technical validation covered signal integrity, numerical checks on feature estimators and completeness of the released files. In every task, the median proportion of recording time retained after preprocessing was 100%. To illustrate reuse, we report saved participant-held-out evaluations comparing XGBoost using all seven families on eight-second windows with a predictor based only on recruitment-group prevalence in the training fold. Recruitment-group discrimination was stronger in Robots than in the three Slackline conditions. These descriptive results from overlapping cohorts do not establish diagnostic validity. The dataset, curation software and aggregate results for the predictive example are publicly available.
