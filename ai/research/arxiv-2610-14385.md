# Deep learning from a control vantage point

## Summary

arXiv:2610.14385v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: This article offers an overview of recent developments in deep learning from a control vantage point. It reviews several classes of models including feedforward and residual neural networks, transformers, large-language models, and selective state space models while explaining how control theory plays an important role in understanding their training, properties, or architecture design. The article explains how residual neural networks can be interpreted as the discretization of a continuous-time control system thereby enabling the use of control theoretic techniques for its analysis. To illustrate the power of this bridge between control and deep learning, two technical results are discussed: 1) the ability of a residual neural network to approximate arbitrary continuous functions is a non-standard controllability problem that can be solved using geometric nonlinear control and 2) transformers, the key architectural component of most large language-models, can be seen the discretization of a consensus algorithm and thus large language-models exhibit consensus. Another point of contact between control theory and deep learning is the connection between backpropagation, the algorithm used to train neural networks, and optimal control. The article addresses how the older connection between these problems, established in the 80's using discrete-time models can be reconciled with the more recent approach using continuous-time models. Several open problems and directions for future research are discussed, including selective state-space models, a recent class of models inspired by the models used in control.
