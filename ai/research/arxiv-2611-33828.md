# Learning a Whole-Body Pivot-Walking Gait for Heavy-Box Transport with a Humanoid

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33828v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Through pivot walking, humans can transport objects that are too heavy to lift. Previous humanoid studies have relied on planned sequences in which object manipulation and stepping are executed separately and the object is brought to rest between successive rotations. However, few studies have developed a single learned controller for humanoid pivot walking. In this paper, we propose a single reinforcement-learning policy that jointly coordinates stepping, balance control, and object manipulation. The policy is trained using rewards that promote alternating pivot motions and a phase reference that progresses according to the displacement of the object. In simulation, we confirmed that the humanoid can pivot a box. For deployment, we fine-tuned the policy into an object-blind version that follows a periodic clock instead of the progress reference. On the real robot, the deployed policy transported the box forward by more than 1 m in all five trials; each trial ended at a base-tilt safety stop. These results demonstrate that coordinated humanoid pivot walking can be learned with reinforcement learning and transferred to a physical humanoid robot without direct object-state sensing.
