# Crossing the Validation Crisis: Cross-Validation Reduces Benchmarking Variance Surprisingly Well

## Summary

arXiv:2606.12552v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Modern machine learning progresses through empirical work, benchmarking new methods to evaluate relative performance. However, the statistical variability inherent in evaluation, exacerbated by the stochastic nature of many algorithms, often makes performance estimation unreliable with the limited test samples available. The result is a validation crisis, in which genuine advances are difficult to discern. Here, we show that doing more cross-validation markedly improves confidence when evaluating and comparing learning algorithm performances. We introduce the concept of sample gain, which quantifies the virtual data augmentation achieved by using multiple cross-validation splits to reduce benchmarking variance. Experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets (histopathologic scans and NLP fine-tuning) demonstrate that multiple splits can substantially improve the reliability and stability of performance estimates, with diminishing returns often setting in later than expected. We also introduce a procedure to dynamically early-stop cross-validation by estimating from the first few splits whether further splits will bring large sample gains. Our findings highlight the value of pushing cross-validation on available samples to achieve robust and reliable benchmarking, and lead to a simple recommendation: split repeatedly.
