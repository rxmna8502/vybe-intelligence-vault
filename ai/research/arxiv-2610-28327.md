# Observation Assimilation Transformer: Probabilistic Weather Analysis from Sparse Station Observations

## Summary

arXiv:2610.28327v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: We present the Observation Assimilation Transformer (OAT), a geometry-aware neural process that corrects and refines a prior numerical weather prediction (NWP) field into a high-resolution posterior field by assimilating sparse observations. OAT is an encoder-decoder transformer that considers a set of observed locations and their NWP prior to condition predictions at a set of arbitrary unobserved query locations. Its sparse attention mechanism makes it scalable to thousands of station observations and depends on relative positions between locations, never on the absolute coordinates. The model allows for partially missing observations, predictions at arbitrary continuous locations without grid projection, and outputs are parametric distributions. A station-randomized training algorithm is developed with continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) loss. We test OAT on two datasets - one at regional scale with hundreds of stations, and one at continental scale with thousands of stations. On these datasets OAT outperforms several baselines and shows well calibrated predictions. In an illustrative case study, OAT's downscaling capabilities are demonstrated by downscaling ERA5 (~31km) to COSMO (1km), where OAT achieves lower mean absolute error (MAE) and CRPS for temperature and precipitation on test stations compared to COSMO.
