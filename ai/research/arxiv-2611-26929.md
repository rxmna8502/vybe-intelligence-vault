# Large Reported CHB-MIT Adaptation Gains Disappear with Pretrained Encoders and Matched Probes

## Summary

arXiv:2611.26929v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Dani and Liebe (2026) (DL) report that adapting the final encoder block of BIOT and CBraMod with self-supervised learning (SSL) improves CHB-MIT seizure-detection ROC-AUC by 36.2 and 29.5 points over linear probing. We investigate these results with their released code and an independent pipeline. The release loads pretrained weights for adaptation only when an otherwise unset configuration key is supplied, and its CBraMod probe reads the reconstruction head with dropout active. With pretrained initialisation and a probe on the encoder output in evaluation mode, the fixed-recipe adaptation gain on DL's split is -0.6 points for BIOT (95% interval [-3.8, +2.6]) and +0.01 for CBraMod ([-0.02, +0.04]) across five seeds. Across DL's split and five subject rotations on CHB-MIT and Siena Scalp EEG, with equally tuned linear probes (30 configurations, up to 500 epochs, validation-based early stopping), mean adaptation effects range from -1.1 to +5.7 ROC-AUC points over 18 comparisons (related settings and feature variants). Twelve of 18 support equivalence within +/-3 points under dependence-adjusted inference (8 of 12 rotation and 4 of 6 fixed-split comparisons); six are unresolved. After correcting rotation intervals for dependence, no positive effect has a 95% interval excluding zero. The +/-3 margin is an analysis threshold, not a clinically validated one, and smaller margins support fewer equivalences. Two fixed-probe gains near +21 points shrink under the same tuning, with smaller benefits unresolved. We also audit a CHB-MIT protocol shared by at least 14 papers. No tested configuration reproduces DL Table 1 as a whole. These findings concern the tested configurations; the encoder-level probe and subsequent checks were chosen post hoc.
