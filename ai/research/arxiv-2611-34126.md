# Out of Sight, Not Out of Clues: Can Video LLMs Guess from Partial Evidence?

## Summary

arXiv:2611.34126v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: In real-world applications like egocentric recording, visual accessibility, and surveillance review, visual evidence for Video Large Language Models (Video LLMs) is often only partially captured. A reliable model should match its response to the evidence: answering when sufficient, guessing from visible clues when partial, and abstaining when absent. Yet existing benchmarks enforce a binary answerable-or-unanswerable split, leaving partial evidence unmeasured and obscuring whether failures arise from missing evidence or misjudging sufficiency. We introduce ClueProbe, a diagnostic benchmark to (1) separate perceiving evidence from judging sufficiency, and (2) validate our diagnosis via targeted fine-tuning. ClueProbe comprises 1,721 human-annotated questions across 598 natural videos, labeled Answerable, Guessable, or Unanswerable, alongside controlled perturbations altering wording or visual input. Evaluating 10 open-source and proprietary Video LLMs reveals that failures stem from judgment rather than perception: models perceive the evidence yet lack graded sufficiency awareness, answering or refusing outright while rarely guessing from partial clues. Targeted fine-tuning shows that this reflects missing supervision rather than insufficient capacity: supervising the guess level lifts appropriate guessing from 1.6% to 40.4% without extra false refusals, whereas conventional refusal tuning rejects 92% of guessable questions. Data and code will be released at https://github.com/suat-llm/ClueProbe.
