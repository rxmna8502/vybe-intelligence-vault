# Spatiotemporal Landslide Runout Reconstruction from Sparse Observations with LAPIS-SHRED

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27132v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Reconstructing the spatiotemporal evolution of landslide runout from limited observations is a central challenge in geohazard assessment: transient flow dynamics are rarely observed directly, and available data are often confined to post-event deposits or brief monitoring windows. We apply LAPIS-SHRED (LAtent Phase Inference from Short time sequence using SHallow REcurrent Decoders) to reconstruct runout events near Mount St. Helens, USA. Trained on physics-based simulation ensembles, the framework supports both backward reconstruction from single frame and forward prediction from short initial window across different runout regimes. Using 32 sensor observations from a single timepoint, LAPIS-SHRED recovers complete runout trajectories with NRMSE below 5.8%, robust across varying observational and training conditions. Validation against observed deposits yields spatial correlation of r = 0.843 against the calibrated simulator's r = 0.853. These results suggest that latent-space temporal inference offers a viable pathway for spatiotemporal landslide reconstruction under extreme observational constraints typical of field settings.
