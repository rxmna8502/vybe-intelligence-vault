# CyberHull: Deterministic Malicious URL Isolation via Convex Hull

## Summary

arXiv:2610.27675v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: The emergence of increasingly sophisticated and evasion-resistant phishing attack strategies represents a significant threat to modern cybersecurity, particularly in cyber-physical systems and internet-based networks. Traditional probabilistic methods and certain machine learning approaches often struggle to detect novel attacks and may lack interpretability or portability. To address these challenges, we introduce CyberHull, a resource-efficient and deterministic framework for identifying malicious URLs through mathematically defined convex hull boundaries across multiple orthogonal feature domains, including structural content entropy, hyperlink graph topology, and semantic token distribution. CyberHull constructs compact convex hulls, referred to as vaults, using the QuickHull algorithm, with a training complexity of O(n log n) under suitable assumptions. URL classification is performed through point-in-polyhedron tests that impose relatively low computational overhead on standard CPUs. Experimental results demonstrate that CyberHull achieves 92 percent detection accuracy, substantially outperforming a baseline linear approach based on Principal Component Analysis (PCA), which achieves 32 percent accuracy. Furthermore, CyberHull offers a computationally efficient alternative to resource-intensive deep learning approaches for malicious URL detection.
