# Learning from Natural Images Untangles Number Sense

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33615v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Numerical cognition is a simple yet fundamental ability that remains challenging for modern vision-language models. Meanwhile, number sense is already present in numerically na\"ive animals, suggesting that it can emerge without explicit numerical learning. Deep neural networks provide a related observation, with numerosity-selective units appearing after training on unrelated natural-image tasks and even before any training. However, these findings leave unresolved whether visual learning contributes beyond the spontaneous emergence of numerical selectivity. Human development provides a clue, as children become better at making judgments in which numerosity and proportion conflict. Here, we introduce a geometric framework to trace how representations of these two quantities change across learning. Although untrained and pretrained networks exhibited similar unit-level numerosity selectivity and basic comparison performance, natural-image pretraining increased the angle between numerosity and proportion subspaces while reducing their overlap, supporting more robust readout under conflict. Direct manipulation of these geometric properties further demonstrated their functional contributions to decoding accuracy and functional independence. Importantly, conventional supervised pretraining was not essential for quantity separation, which also emerged through self-supervised learning without category labels and synthetic-image training without natural-image exposure. Together, our findings suggest that structured visual learning can refine spontaneously emerging number sense into robust and functionally independent quantity representations without explicit numerical training.
