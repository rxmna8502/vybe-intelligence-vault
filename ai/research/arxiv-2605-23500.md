# GRTO: Bootstrapped Group Relative Tool Optimization for Referring Segmentation

## Summary

arXiv:2605.23500v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Referring segmentation requires reasoning to connect language with visual evidence and identify the intended target in challenging scenarios. This makes GRPO a natural choice for training VLMs together with a segmentation tool. However, existing approaches optimize only the VLM, assuming that the tool is perfect and leaving it untouched. This assumption is particularly limiting when the tool faces domain shift, as segmentation errors can undermine even correct reasoning. We therefore argue that the segmentation tool should be optimized together with the reasoning policy. However, this is challenging because policy optimization and differentiable tool learning rely on fundamentally different objectives. In this work, we introduce group relative tool optimization (GRTO), a mathematically grounded two-stage framework for jointly optimizing a policy and its differentiable tool. The key idea is to first align the tool with the policy expected after joint training, rather than the initial policy. The prealigned tool then leads to a more robust reward signal and superior policy convergence in subsequent policy training. In the first stage, GRTO bootstraps the tool toward the theoretically optimal policy, capturing readily attainable gains without costly on-policy rollouts. This alignment bootstraps the second stage, where GRTO extends GRPO to jointly refine the policy and tool. Across three challenging referring segmentation benchmarks, GRTO achieves substantial improvements over plain GRPO and alternative tool training procedures, matching or surpassing domain-specific state-of-the-art methods. This demonstrates the value of unifying reinforcement learning with differentiable auxiliary objectives for reasoning-intensive segmentation.
