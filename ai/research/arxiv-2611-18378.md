# Structure makes quantum circuits trainable

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18378v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: The scalability of variational quantum algorithms is limited by barren plateaus, where gradients vanish exponentially in system size. This worst-case result raises fundamental questions about the trainability of quantum AI models. We present a major advance towards resolving this trainability question by answering whether a given quantum circuit has a barren plateau. We do this using an analytical framework that bounds the gradient variance for arbitrary input states $\rho$ and observables $\mathcal{M}$ by decomposing the unitary representation of the associated compact Lie group. When the circuit segments on both sides of a gate form $2$-designs, we obtain the variance exactly and prove a sufficient condition for trainability: if $\rho^{\otimes 2}$ and $\mathcal{M}^{\otimes 2}$ are sufficiently concentrated on the same irreducible invariant subspace of polynomial dimension, and the differentiated gate moves this subspace by an inverse-polynomial amount, then the gradient variance is at least inverse-polynomial. We further present a sampling algorithm that estimates the gradient variance from runs of the ansatz, without constructing the representation-theoretic projectors. Our algorithm decides whether the variance exceeds a tolerance $\tau$ with the number of runs polynomial in $1/\tau$, including cases in which the bounds are inconclusive. This resolves the question of the trainability of the circuit before expensive training procedures.
