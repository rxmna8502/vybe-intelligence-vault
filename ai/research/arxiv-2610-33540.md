# Patch-Derived or Register Tokens? Comparing Variable-Length Image Tokenizers

## Summary

arXiv:2610.33540v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Image tokenizers convert pixels into compact latent sequences for efficient generative modeling. Most approaches arrange latents on a downsampled two-dimensional grid, tying sequence length to image resolution. We compare two variable-length strategies: computationally efficient patch-derived tokens, which inherit explicit spatial organization, and more demanding register tokens, which have no predefined spatial layout. Our reconstruction results reveal that patch-derived tokens perform substantially better under extreme compression, whereas register tokens achieve slightly better reconstruction quality at intermediate budgets. At high token budgets, both approaches achieve similar reconstruction quality. For downstream generative tasks, register tokens outperform patch-derived tokens. Adding representation alignment with external pretrained visual encoders substantially improves the generative performance of patch-derived tokens, allowing them to match or outperform register tokens depending on the token budget. We further find that both approaches learn spatially localized representations. Patch-derived tokens largely retain their explicitly imposed spatial organization, while register tokens develop an implicit two-dimensional spatial structure despite having no predefined layout. Representation alignment reduces this spatial organizational constraint, enabling individual tokens to exert a stronger non-local influence.
