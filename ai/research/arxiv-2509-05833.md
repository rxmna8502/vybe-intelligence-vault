# Benchmarking Robust Aggregation in Decentralized Gradient Marketplaces

## Summary

arXiv:2509.05833v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Institutions that cannot centralize or sell raw data increasingly exchange model updates instead: cross-silo federated learning is already in production among hospital networks and pharmaceutical consortia. A growing line of research targets the next step: marketplaces in which gradient updates are exchanged as paid, query-specific data products, though none is yet deployed at scale. We formalize this emerging paradigm as a Gradient Data Marketplace (GDM), in which data consumers issue learning tasks and distributed participants contribute query-specific gradient updates. Federated learning asks whether the model survives an attack; a marketplace must also ask who got paid. Existing protocols ensure model-level robustness but fail to capture whether high-quality data is correctly identified and rewarded. In a GDM, selection determines payment, incentivizing participants to optimize for inclusion rather than utility. We introduce a modular benchmarking framework that separates mechanism integrity (whether selection and payment reward true utility) from model integrity, and evaluate a standard baseline alongside 11 widely used aggregation mechanisms on five real-world vision, text, and tabular datasets. Our results reveal consistent adverse selection: honest high-variance contributions are rejected as outliers while strategically homogeneous malicious contributions are accepted and rewarded, even when model performance remains stable. These failures are not artifacts of our accounting: they persist under continuous, weight-proportional payment, compound across repeated market rounds as underpaid honest sellers exit, and worsen when honest sellers add differential-privacy noise, which the filters misread as contamination. Our benchmark provides a foundation for designing incentive-aware, auditable data selection mechanisms in emerging data marketplaces.
