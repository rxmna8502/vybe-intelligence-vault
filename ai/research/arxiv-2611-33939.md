# CondUQ: Calibrated Conditional Uncertainty Quantification Using Distribution Matching

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33939v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Many applications, especially reliability- and safety-critical ones, require accurate uncertainty quantification for the outputs of model- or learning-based measurement, estimation, and prediction systems. The errors of such systems often vary with factors such as input quality and operating conditions, and thus cannot be accurately described by a single Gaussian model. Although Gaussian mixture models and calibrated quantiles can approximate such error distributions, their unconditional forms do not capture how the error behavior depends on these factors. Hence, we propose condUQ, an approach that conditions error models on observable factors for enabling accurate and reliable uncertainty quantification. condUQ is built on top of an existing system without modifying it and applies even when the system is a black box. First, condUQ maps the observable factors to a latent quality index (QI) such that errors sharing the same QI follow a common, approximately Gaussian conditional distribution. The QI predictor is trained end-to-end with a distribution-matching objective, without requiring quality labels. Second, for each QI, a Gaussian error model is then fitted and calibrated on a large held-out dataset. condUQ is demonstrated on three examples: (a) GPS multipath errors conditioned on satellite elevation and signal strength, (b) relative attitude errors of a transformer-based visual relative pose regression model conditioned on its internal features of the image pairs, and (c) prediction errors of a world model conditioned on the input observations and actions. In all three examples, condUQ substantially reduces the discrepancy between the modeled and empirical error distributions compared with a single global model. Moreover, the learned QIs reveal how the underlying factors affect the error characteristics in a manner consistent with physical principles.
