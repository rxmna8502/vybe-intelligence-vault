# FairITR: Fairness-Aware Learning of Multicategory Individualized Treatment Rules

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27894v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Individualized treatment rules (ITRs) recommend treatment decisions based on individual characteristics and can improve treatment outcomes beyond traditional one-size-fits-all strategies. However, similar to other commonly used algorithmic decision-making methods, ITRs without considering model fairness may result in unfair decisions across groups defined by sensitive attributes, such as race or gender. Although extensive methods have been developed for ITR estimation, most focus solely on optimizing treatment outcomes, while research on fairness-aware ITRs remains limited. Moreover, the practical scenario of multicategory treatments is rarely considered in existing fair ITR studies. To address these gaps, we introduce a fairness notion for multicategory ITRs that requires treatment decisions to be independent of sensitive attributes, and then formulate the corresponding problem of estimating fair ITRs. To solve this problem, we further propose FairITR, a fairness-aware learning framework that jointly optimizes treatment outcomes and fairness. FairITR builds on angle-based classification for multicategory ITR learning and integrates adversarial learning with covariance-based regularization to mitigate unfairness in treatment decisions. A theoretical justification for the fairness properties induced by the adversarial debiasing mechanism is also provided. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets show that FairITR substantially reduces unfairness in ITRs while maintaining competitive treatment values relative to existing baselines.
