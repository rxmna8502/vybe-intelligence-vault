# LTM: Latent Transition Based In-Context Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models

## Summary

arXiv:2610.21362v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Robotic manipulation often requires remembering interactions that are no longer visible. We introduce Latent Transition Memory (LTM), which stores history as compact latent transitions between consecutive observations. Instantiated on $\pi_{0.5}$ as LTM-$\pi$, our method uses a frozen latent action encoder, a lightweight adapter, and causal KV caching for incremental inference without memory-specific semantic annotations or external reasoning-model calls. With DreamDojo transitions, LTM-$\pi$ achieves 58.54% success on RoboMME and 80.5% on LIBERO-Mem, performing best among baselines without external reasoning models or memory-specific annotations. Ablations support the contribution of transition content beyond temporal cues and the efficiency of delayed memory admission. Scaling RoboMME demonstrations tenfold improves success from 57.67% to 87.50%, while a Cup Swap diagnostic highlights the role of process supervision in temporal composition. Under a 200-token memory budget, success remains 84.00%, versus 87.50% without compression. Finally, real-robot counting success improves from 11.25% to 82.50%. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness, scalability, and efficiency of latent transition memory, highlighting its capacity for complex temporal information, bounded-context operation, and real-world robotic control.
