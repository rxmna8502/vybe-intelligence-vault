# Proof-Grounded Patient-Specific Clinical Explanations from Knowledge-Graph Reasoning

## Summary

arXiv:2610.06549v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Clinical decision support can lack auditable links from observations and encoded knowledge to conclusions and recommendations. Downstream of a frozen, training-free clinical knowledge-graph reasoner, the CKG Clinical Explanation Engine converts patient inference states and disease knowledge into typed facts, explicit rule traces, provenance-linked conclusions, and policy-licensed recommendations. Separate availability, representation completeness, and disease-specific activation distinguish observed zero-activation and partial evidence from unobserved evidence. Language-generation evaluation remains future work. Across six usable workbooks (11,289 patients, 33,867 patient-disease traces, 1,715,928 feature-evidence rows), IG-range validity, knowledge provenance, cross-sheet numerical fidelity (203,202/203,202), and exported fact/report field presence (33,867/33,867) reached 100.00%. Availability-representation consistency reached 99.29% (1,703,811/1,715,928), with 12,117 disagreements across five systematic feature-cohort patterns. Observed zero-activation and partial instances numbered 111,488 and 226,880, respectively. A separate seeded 30-patient end-to-end audit ran failure-free, passing all prespecified trace, licensing, provenance, and state-consistency checks. Paired comparisons (90 traces, fixed inherited diagnostic scores and policy catalogue) showed the availability-only baseline changed every report but no action sets. State collapse changed 20 action sets and removed 10 urgent-review occurrences. Rule replay reproduced all 40 action changes from reclassifying partial evidence as unobserved. The bounded validator detected all 450 single-fault mutants. Structural and implementation auditability were established. Clinical correctness and utility remain unestablished.
