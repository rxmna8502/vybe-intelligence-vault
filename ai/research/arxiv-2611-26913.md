# Surface Cues Over Structure: Probing LLM Behavior on Brain Connectivity Graphs

## Summary

arXiv:2611.26913v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) provides a noninvasive view of functional interactions across the brain, which can be represented as connectivity graphs for neurological disease analysis. Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being paired with such representations, yet it remains unclear whether they genuinely reason over the underlying connectivity or simply respond to surface-level cues that may trigger learned priors. We probe this question through a controlled study of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) diagnosis from resting-state functional connectivity, isolating one factor at a time to determine what drives an LLM's predictions on brain connectivity graphs. Across six models, from small open-weight LLMs to a proprietary one, the same pattern holds: the results provide little evidence that predictions are consistently grounded in subject-specific connectivity. Brain region names, numeric formatting, and prompt framing shift what a model predicts without improving how well it separates ADHD subjects from Controls; perturbing the graph changes predictions no more for clinically meaningful regions than for random ones; and the models' own justifications contradict the values they cite, inconsistently interpreting the connectivity evidence provided to them. Across every probe, predictions follow the model's built-in class bias and the surface form of the prompt rather than the subject's connectivity, calling into question a premise underlying current LLM brain integration work. Our code is available at https://github.com/Roy025/ADHD-code.
