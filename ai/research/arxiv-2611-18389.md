# A systematic comparison of physics-informed neural networks and mechanistic models for epidemic modeling and forecasting

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18389v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have recently been applied to epidemic modeling and forecasting with promising results. These methods integrate mechanistic knowledge with data-driven learning. Yet, their comparative performance against well-established, and simpler epidemic models is underexplored. Here, we conduct a systematic comparison of a PINN-based framework and a stochastic mechanistic model. Both approaches share the same age-structured SEIRD core and rolling-window forecasting pipeline, in a deliberately minimal setting. We evaluate their retrospective fits and probabilistic forecasts in two synthetic scenarios and the first COVID-19 wave in London. Interestingly, while PINNs underperform in synthetic scenarios, they achieve better performance on real data. Furthermore, PINNs provide narrower uncertainty intervals and sharper estimation of parameters in synthetic scenarios. Our findings confirm PINNs as a promising tool in epidemiology. At the same time, the results reaffirm the value of simpler approaches such as traditional compartmental models, and promote additional benchmarking studies.
