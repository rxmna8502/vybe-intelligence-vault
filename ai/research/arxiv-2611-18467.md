# Score-based neural ordinary differential equations for reaction-diffusions

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18467v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: A diffusion equation can be rewritten as a transport equation driven by the score function of the evolving density. Conventional probability-flow formulations, which move particles deterministically with this score-driven velocity, typically conserve total mass. In this work, we develop a Lagrangian method for reaction-diffusion equations in which each particle carries a position and a physical weight. The characteristic ODEs incorporate source terms into the evolution of the log-density and its gradient, while particle weights track changes in mass. We combine this representation with a learned velocity field and weighted particle training to approximate these dynamics. For non-increasing reaction rates, a relative-entropy inequality supports the mass-normalized self-consistency residual as the training objective. This objective can control the error of the induced density when the growth rate is bounded. The algorithm divides the time interval into short windows. In each window, a frozen velocity generates the particle data for a regression of the network velocity. We draw the particles from period-adapted initial sampling distributions and weight them in the loss partly by their predicted future mass, because the reaction can make regions with little initial mass important later. We numerically investigate the method on the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) equation with a linear source term, Fisher-KPP equations, and Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao gradient flows.
