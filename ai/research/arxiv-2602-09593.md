# Why Does Counterintuitive Likelihood Behavior Rarely Emerge in Tabular Anomaly Detection?

## Summary

arXiv:2602.09593v3 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Deep generative models with tractable likelihoods, exemplified by normalizing flows, offer an effective basis for anomaly detection through likelihood-based scoring. We demonstrate that, unlike in the image domain where deep generative models frequently assign higher likelihoods to anomalous data, such counterintuitive behavior occurs rarely in tabular settings. We first introduce a domain-agnostic formulation that enables consistent detection and evaluation of the counterintuitive phenomenon, addressing the lack of a precise definition in prior work. Through extensive experiments on 47 tabular datasets benchmarked against 13 baseline models and on 10 CV/NLP embedding datasets from ADBench, we show that the phenomenon is consistently rare in general tabular data and that normalizing flow likelihood achieves competitive anomaly detection performance. We further provide theoretical and empirical analyses attributing this disparity to differences in dimensionality and feature correlation between tabular and image data. Therefore, our results indicate that likelihood-based detection with normalizing flows is a practical approach for tabular anomaly detection.
