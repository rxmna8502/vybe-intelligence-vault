# Arbitrary-Accuracy Neural Approximation with Optimal Neuron Count and Optimal Bit Complexity

## Summary

arXiv:2609.35628v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: We study the minimum number of hidden neurons required for arbitrary-accuracy approximation of continuous functions on $[0,1]^d$ and the associated encoding complexity. For $d\geq 2$, we construct two fixed, explicitly defined activation functions and, for each, a closed-form network with two hidden layers of widths $d$ and $1$ that achieves arbitrary accuracy in the uniform norm. We prove that $d+1$ is the exact minimum total number of hidden neurons among standard feedforward networks with locally integrable activations and affine outputs. We further give a simpler construction using a single elementary activation that combines the floor and exponential functions. This construction requires three hidden layers of widths $d$, $1$, and $2$. If a skip connection is allowed, widths $d$, $1$, and $1$ suffice. For a bounded $\alpha$-H\"older class with $\alpha\in(0,1]$, the networks with the digit-extraction activation and with the elementary activation require $O(\varepsilon^{-d/\alpha}\log(1/\varepsilon))$ bits, matching the metric-entropy lower bound up to a logarithmic factor. The other $(d,1)$ network, which stores differences of neighboring values along a spanning tree, needs only $\Theta(\varepsilon^{-d/\alpha})$ bits and is optimal in both neuron count and bit complexity.
