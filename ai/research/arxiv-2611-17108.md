# LazyWeights: Decode-on-Demand Edge LLMs

## Summary

arXiv:2611.17108v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Memory determines which large language models (LLMs) an edge device can run and how fast it runs them: capacity limits a 4 GB Jetson Nano, and bandwidth limits an 8 GB Jetson Orin Nano Super. Deployed weight formats (GPTQ, AWQ, and the GGUF K- and I-quants) spend the same number of bits on every integer code of a matrix, although the codes are far from uniform. We present LazyWeights, which keeps these formats, tunes GPTQ so the codes compress, Huffman-codes them losslessly in interleaved lanes, and decodes them on demand in a small unit between DRAM and L2, so GPU kernels run unchanged and DRAM moves only coded bytes. At 1% and 3.5% perplexity budgets, coded Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B and SmolLM-1.7B are 5.7-31.4% smaller than the best GGUF format. A bit-exact 0.21 mm^2 ASIC decoder unit (1 GHz, 7 nm) cuts Orin latency and energy per token by up to 36% and 38% (54% and 44% with prompt-lookup speculation). On the Nano, LazyWeights needs 58-64% less time and 53-57% less energy per token than the GGUF runtime on the CPU, and runs a streamed Mistral-7B 6.6x faster at 3.89 bits per weight. A Huffman decoder beats an rANS decoder in rate and area at equal throughput, and the unit runs bit-exact on a Zynq UltraScale+ FPGA (AMD ZCU102).
