# Temporal Diffusion Models for Joint Signal-Channel Estimation under Gaussian and $\alpha$-Stable MIMO Channels

## Summary

arXiv:2611.22548v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Diffusion models have emerged as powerful tools for learning complex data distributions and solving inverse problems. However, most diffusion models rely on the Gaussian distribution, which does not accurately represent heavy-tailed data. In this work, we study diffusion-based reconstruction under Gaussian and symmetric $\alpha$-stable fading channels, while also exploring the benefits of using possible temporal dependence in the channel. We consider two variants: pilot-based channel-only estimation and joint image-channel recovery for MIMO systems. Our approach is based on several standard and score-based diffusion models for both Gaussian and $\alpha$-stable distributions. We refine these models by guiding the reverse diffusion process using the observation likelihood and show how a single trained diffusion model can be reused for the location-scale family of distributions without retraining. In blind MIMO recovery, separate diffusion models are used for the image and channel, and their estimates are combined through likelihood guidance. For each setting, we compare independent and temporal reconstruction, where information from previous observations is used to improve the estimation. We show that, under a time-dependent channel model, such a temporal reconstruction improves channel estimation and the quality of the reconstructed image. Finally, we show that, for the same heavy-tailed channel data, using an $\alpha$-stable diffusion prior provides better channel estimation and image reconstruction than using a Gaussian diffusion prior.
