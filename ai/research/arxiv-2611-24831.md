# Overlooked and Underutilized: Prototypes are the Missing Piece in Tabular XAI

## Summary

arXiv:2611.24831v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Explainable AI (XAI) is frequently applied to high-stakes tabular data, yet the field relies heavily on feature attribution methods. While these approaches offer technical transparency, evaluating dozens of isolated features demands excessive cognitive effort from non-expert users. In this paper, we argue that prototypes remain overlooked and underutilized, and should be integrated as a complementary, intuitive gateway to auditing complex machine learning models. Although entire exemplars function as an intuitive baseline for prototype-based explanations, we highlight prototypical parts as particularly valuable for tabular data. By grouping related features into bounded subspaces, prototypical parts technically embody human-understandable concepts, replacing isolated features with cohesive semantic units. We support this integration through two primary lenses. Cognitively, these parts align with Rosch's categorization theory and Kahneman's System 1 thinking to naturally reduce cognitive load for humans. Then, we introduce a Geometric Validity Triad to conceptualize why prototypes validate the data manifold - a critical dimension often neglected by existing methods. We contend that making tabular XAI genuinely comprehensible requires shifting toward composite explanation pipelines that pair intuitive similarity-based reasoning with rigorous feature-level auditing. The paper concludes by outlining open challenges of prototype-based methods and discussing potential solutions to guide future research.
