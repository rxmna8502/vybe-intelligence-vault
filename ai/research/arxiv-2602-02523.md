# TabularMath: Evaluating Computational Extrapolation in Tabular Learning via Program-Verified Synthesis

## Summary

arXiv:2602.02523v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Standard tabular benchmarks reward interpolation: predicting values inside the data manifold, where local statistical smoothing works. However, much high-value tabular data, from pricing and financial rules to physical simulation, is produced by deterministic computations rather than noisy relationships. Can tabular models move from statistical interpolation to computational extrapolation, returning exact answers even beyond the range seen in training? We introduce TabularMath, a benchmark of 114 deterministic computations (233,472 rows) compiled from GSM8K and AIME problems into verified programs. We evaluate nine tabular methods, closed-form and symbolic regression baselines, and in-context learning with GPT-OSS-120B. On regression metrics, TabPFN v2.5 looks excellent: with 128 rows its median $R^2$ is 0.999 in-distribution, and it is the only tabular method whose median $R^2$ stays positive (0.76) when nearly every answer exceeds the training range. Exact answers tell a different story. Out of distribution (OOD), its accuracy falls from 39.5% to 7.8%, it answers no query correctly in 70 of 100 GSM8K tasks, and sixteen times more rows raise its OOD accuracy by only 0.4 points. Yet the answers are in the table: by construction, every label is a program's output on the row's inputs, and the training rows typically suffice to recover that program. Symbolic regression on the numbers alone recovers every OOD answer in 72 of 100 GSM8K tasks, and in-context learning with GPT-OSS-120B answers 39.2% of OOD queries. Tabular foundation models fit the trend but miss the computation, an inductive-bias gap that more data does not close. The competition-level AIME tasks remain open for every method.
