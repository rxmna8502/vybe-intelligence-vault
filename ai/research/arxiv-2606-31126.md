# Can Tabular In-Context Learners Generalize to Biomolecular Property Prediction?

## Summary

arXiv:2606.31126v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Predicting biomolecular properties from limited labeled data is a central bottleneck in protein engineering and small-molecule design. As strong pretrained encoders now supply rich fixed-length representations, the difficulty has shifted from representation learning to building a data-efficient predictor for the few-shot regime. Tabular foundation models such as TabPFN and TabICL perform this prediction through in-context inference after pretraining on synthetic tables. We test whether they can act as flexible interpolators over informative fixed biomolecular representations. Treating each method as a predictor-representation pair, we evaluate across two domains. On ProteinGym, TabPFN3-ESMC600M is competitive with leading supervised-substitution entries and obtains the lowest MSE among the leaderboard-context rows considered, while Kermut remains stronger by Spearman. Performance is substantially lower under modulo and contiguous holdouts than under random splits. On the five-endpoint PpEST dataset, tabular models show favorable descriptive performance against matched supervised baselines, but the random split measures within-family interpolation and the small endpoint count precludes strong model-ordering claims. For small-molecule classification with ECFP/RDKit descriptors, no single predictor-representation pairing dominates across TDC ADMET, MoleculeNet, FS-Mol, and DrugOOD, but several pairings are competitive with strong descriptor and graph baselines. Crucially, on both protein and small-molecule few-shot tasks, these predictor-representation pairs offer strong performance. We conclude that tabular foundation models can be strong biomolecular predictors, but only when coupled with expressive representations.
