# What Should World Models Forget? Stratified Retention for Continual Adaptation

## Summary

arXiv:2610.03713v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Continual learning treats any drop in performance on previously seen data as forgetting, a convention inherited from settings where the prediction target is stationary. World models violate this assumption: their target is the environment, which changes, so knowledge that was accurate when learned can later become false, and revising it is correct behavior rather than a defect. Non-stationary ground truth is well studied in the concept drift literature and in the temporal factuality of language models, but it has not been formulated for world models, which are distinctive in that they also encode structural knowledge, such as physical laws and object permanence, that must never be degraded. We argue that continual world models need retention stratified by invariance timescale, the time over which knowledge stays true: invariants should be refined but never degraded, while instance-level facts should be revised once the environment changes. We show that standard forgetting metrics, used alone, count correct revision as forgetting and so rank a frozen model highest. We propose differential retention, which reports two quantities separately: invariant retention, checked after every adaptation round, and revision latency, the number of observations a model needs to pick up a change.
