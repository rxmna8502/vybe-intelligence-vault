# EgoSurgHands: An Egocentric 3D Hand Pose Dataset & Benchmark for Open Surgery Training

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27101v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Surgical skill assessment, AR guidance, and robot learning from demonstration all benefit from 3D tracking of the surgeon's hands, yet no surgical dataset offers real egocentric articulated 3D hand pose. We introduce EgoSurgHands, a benchmark for real-captured egocentric hand pose estimation in open surgery training on synthetic pads. Captured with multi-sensor glasses, it contains 41,909 training hand-instance rows across 55 procedure recordings and 9,476 human-validated evaluation rows across 13 held-out recordings, spanning 13 tasks, 3 glove colors, and 4 trainees from first-year medical student to third-year resident. Each frame carries a multi-sensor stack: intrinsics, hand landmarks with per-hand confidence, timestamps, and eye gaze, plus SLAM extrinsics where SLAM succeeded. Evaluation labels combine sensor-fused pseudo-ground truth, human cross-review of every frame, surgeon adjudication of flagged cases, and an independent multi-view triangulated reference. Alongside the within-corpus, procedure-disjoint split, a leave-one-wearer-out protocol tested unseen trainees. Across eight methods evaluated zero-shot, from MANO-based reconstructors to the two-hand IntagHand and the keypoint-only MediaPipe Hands, egocentric pretraining was associated with stronger transfer on every held-out wearer. WildHands and JointTransformer had 18\% and 40\% lower PA-MPJPE, respectively, than the non-egocentric HaMeR. JointTransformer was the best performing model with an error of 14.95 mm. A substantial surgical domain gap remains, and the widely deployed MediaPipe Hands detected a hand in only 24.1\% of evaluation rows. As a baseline, a 58K-parameter residual corrector (JRN) on frozen backbones reached 10.96-14.85 mm PA-MPJPE on held-out wearers for the two ego-pretrained backbones. We release the dataset and benchmark code at https://egosurghands.github.io
