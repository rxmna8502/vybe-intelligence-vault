# QSTAR: Quantum Selective Transfer with Adaptive Routing

## Summary

arXiv:2607.21411v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Quantum transfer learning (QTL) is commonly evaluated by replacing a classical classifier with a fixed variational quantum head, leaving unclear when quantum inference provides measurable benefit. We propose QSTAR: Quantum Selective Transfer with Adaptive Routing, which retains high-confidence classical predictions and routes low-confidence samples to a quantum fallback. Using a frozen ResNet18 backbone, we compare manually designed QTL heads, architecture-searched KetGPT heads, and parameter-matched classical baselines under consistent experimental settings. On Fashion-MNIST, standard QTL heads reach at most 59.7% accuracy and 0.564 F1-score, whereas compact KetGPT Candidate #160 reaches 80.2% accuracy and 0.800 F1-score. On the routed low-confidence subset, Candidate #160 improves accuracy over the matched MLP by 6.31, 5.38, and 3.73 percentage points at \tau=0.70, 0.80, and 0.90, respectively. At the selected operating point \tau=0.70, the adaptive system reaches 81.78% accuracy and 0.816 F1-score while routing only 36.12% of test samples to the quantum branch. Candidate #160 uses 10 quantum parameters and 9 gates, while experiments on CIFAR-10, KMNIST, and SVHN show consistent accuracy gains over the corresponding adaptive classical baselines. These results show that selective quantum invocation can reduce quantum execution while improving inference on low-confidence samples.
