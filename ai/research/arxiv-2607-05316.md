# How Much is Left? LLMs Linearly Encode Their Remaining Output Length

## Summary

arXiv:2607.05316v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Large Language models generate tokens, without explicitly keeping track of the length of the generated text. This paper asks the question: can we estimate the number of tokens remaining in a generated response, using just a linear probe on the model's hidden states? Previous work on inference scheduling established that length is statistically predictable, using nonlinear methods. This paper contributes a linear representation method, using frozen hidden states of 7-8B instruction-tuned models, across seven datasets. We show that total completion length can be estimated, with performance stronger than statistical baselines, and stronger than the fair comparison to the prediction at the last token of the prompt. On the natural-language datasets, the probe's error lies between 0.57 and 0.88 of the statistical baseline's. Interestingly, when a retraction token occurs ("Wait"), the probe predicted length jumps upward by over 70 tokens, on average, compared to the baseline, where it drops by 0.7, on average. Moreover, the direction found can be used for length steering: adding to the probe direction increases completion length and vice-versa, on Llama-3.1-8B / GSM8K. These results indicate that LLMs have a linearly decodable internal representation of output length.
