# QuaNAS: Uncertainty-Aware Reuse of Earlier Searches for Query-Efficient Neural Architecture Search

## Summary

arXiv:2610.31251v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Neural architecture search (NAS) on a new dataset often affords only tens of short training runs, so what the search knows before its first evaluation decides its efficiency. Yet most NAS methods start from scratch, guided at best by zero-cost proxies that are unreliable across tasks. QuaNAS treats a library of earlier searches in the same search space as explicit knowledge of uncertain reliability. Main effects of design decisions learned from this library and, secondarily, zero-cost proxies become priors over candidate architectures without any label of the new dataset. A Gaussian process starts at the prior optimum and re-estimates after every evaluation how far each prior is trusted. Under a protocol frozen before any confirmation data were seen, QuaNAS was evaluated once on 12 new image, time-series, text and tabular datasets. It lowered anytime regret on the short-training objective 2.7- to 3.9-fold relative to five pre-specified NAS baselines that start from scratch (Holm-adjusted p = 0.0012 each). Its final models matched (non-inferiority margin 0.5 pp) or exceeded matched-recipe reference models on nine datasets, as did the prior optimum, selected without a single evaluation. Post-hoc comparisons with established transfer methods given the same library trace the gain to the library: without it, the same Gaussian process had over twice the regret. They also show where each transfer mechanism works best: the pooled main effects of QuaNAS for weakly transferable tabular tasks, per-task surrogates for image, time-series and text tasks and for small or mismatched libraries. Finally, the main effects make the transferred knowledge inspectable.
