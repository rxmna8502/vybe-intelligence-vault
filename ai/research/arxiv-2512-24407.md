# Efficient Inference for Inverse Reinforcement Learning and Dynamic Discrete Choice Models

## Summary

arXiv:2512.24407v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: In many sequential decision-making problems, researchers observe actions but not the rewards that drive behavior, yet wish to evaluate counterfactual policies. Flexible inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) methods accommodate rich reward representations but typically lack valid inference, whereas classical dynamic discrete choice (DDC) methods provide inference under restrictive parametric structure. We develop a semiparametric framework for debiased inverse reinforcement learning in maximum-entropy IRL and Gumbel-shock DDC models. We show that the log behavior policy acts as a pseudo-reward, identifying policy-value differences without point identifying the reward and, under a normalization, yielding a point-identified reward representative. This reduces inference on reward-dependent estimands to inference on smooth functionals of the behavior policy and transition kernel. We establish pathwise differentiability and characterize efficient influence functions through reward and transition Riesz representers, with the transition component obtainable from existing observed-reward off-policy evaluation theory. For normalized rewards, we derive an explicit transformation of these representers that accounts for the normalization constraint. We construct automatic debiased machine-learning estimators that permit flexible nuisance estimation while attaining $\sqrt{n}$-consistency, asymptotic normality, and semiparametric efficiency. We illustrate the theory through efficient inference for fixed-policy values under pseudo-rewards and normalized rewards, and for counterfactual values when the agent reoptimizes over a restricted action set.
