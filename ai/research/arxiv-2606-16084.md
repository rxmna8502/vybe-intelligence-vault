# Rhythm of the Deep: Two-tier acoustic organization of sperm-whale codas from click waveforms to coda sequences

## Summary

arXiv:2606.16084v5 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Sperm-whale codas are usually described by the number and timing of their clicks. These measures reveal patterns in whale communication, but do not describe how individual clicks sound. We ask what click waveforms add beyond timing and how acoustic information is organized within codas and across sequences of codas. We analyze 1,952 codas, using frozen audio encoders to identify recurring forms of clicks and whole codas independently. Direct spectral and envelope measurements then test whether the click forms differ in recorded sound. Within codas, click-form composition improves the measured prediction of whole-coda form beyond timing alone. Click spacing also remains related to whole-coda sound when the same click forms are present. Neighboring click forms help predict the next click in the annotated sample, although their order does not improve the tested classification of whole codas. Across recorded sequences, the second preceding whole-coda form improves prediction beyond the immediately preceding form. The gain exceeds two broad first-order references, but stricter controls do not establish a general higher-order rule. Together, these findings show different organizing relationships at the two scales. Within codas, click sound and rhythmic spacing help distinguish complete vocalizations. Across codas, earlier whole-coda forms help predict what follows.
