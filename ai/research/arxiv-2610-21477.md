# Tool-Augmented Large Language Models for Reliable Medical Calculation from Patient Notes

## Summary

arXiv:2610.21477v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Medical calculators translate raw patient observations into clinically actionable values and scores. However, applying these calculators directly to free-form patient notes requires precise variable extraction combined with the exact execution of complex formulas, scoring rules, and output conventions. Relying solely on direct large language model (LLM) prompting is unreliable in this context, as rule recall, parameter extraction, arithmetic reasoning, and formatting become entangled within a single generative step. To address these limitations, we propose a tool-augmented LLM framework for reliable medical calculation from patient notes. The framework retrieves and structures calculator specifications, generates reusable executable tools, and uses a constrained extractor to map each patient note to calculator-ready inputs. The tools then perform deterministic calculation, separating clinical language understanding from rule execution and arithmetic. Evaluated on the MedCalc-Bench dataset, our method achieves 86.73% final-answer accuracy, outperforming the one-shot prompting and supervised fine tuning-only baselines by 24.0 and 17.27 percentage points. Ablation and error analyses show that these performance gains are primarily driven by rule-grounded tool synthesis and schema-constrained extraction, whereas remaining failures are concentrated in clinical-semantic extraction and benchmark-specific output contracts. By grounding calculator logic in executable tools, the proposed approach alleviates arithmetic hallucinations and improves the computational reliability of the final clinical outputs.
