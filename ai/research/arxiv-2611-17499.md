# Continuous Cuffless Blood Pressure Estimation Using PPG From Methods to Clinical Translation

## Summary

arXiv:2611.17499v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Continuous blood pressure (BP) monitoring could improve hypertension management by capturing changes that intermittent cuff measurements miss. Photoplethysmography (PPG) is widely explored for cuffless BP estimation because optical sensors are inexpensive, wearable, and already present in many consumer and clinical devices. PPG, however, does not measure arterial pressure: BP is inferred from waveform characteristics or pulse timing, which also depend on vascular tone, arterial stiffness, sensor contact, motion, and individual physiology. This survey reviews PPG-based cuffless BP estimation along the full measurement chain, covering physiological mechanisms, waveform features, pulse arrival time (PAT), pulse transit time (PTT), machine-learning methods, datasets, calibration, validation, deployment, bias, and clinical translation. Studies are assessed by the strength of evidence they provide rather than by reported error alone, with particular attention to subject and temporal leakage, dataset imbalance, calibration dependence and drift, motion artifacts, and the gap between benchmark accuracy and reliable real-world measurement. The review finds that progress depends on more than better prediction algorithms. Future work should prioritize physiologically informed models, diverse longitudinal datasets, transparent reporting of calibration requirements and failure rates, stronger evaluation protocols, and studies that demonstrate practical value in clinical care.
