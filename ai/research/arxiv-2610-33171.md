# Learning the Past from the Future: Retrospective Reconstruction in Temporal Interaction Networks

## Summary

arXiv:2610.33171v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Temporal link prediction uses past interactions to predict future links. In practice, interaction records from web and mobile applications can contain gaps caused by logging failures, API limits or lost records, even when later events remain available. To our knowledge, the extent to which later events help recover interactions within such gaps has not been measured directly for this ranking task. We study retrospective reconstruction using the tgbl-wiki and tgbl-comment datasets. We hide a two-hour window, use its interactions as ground truth, and rank the directed pairs active within it. We compare count-based heuristics and logistic regression models using past information alone or together with 1, 6 or 24 hours of later events. Target pairs and candidate lists remain fixed across conditions, so differences in ranking performance cannot be attributed to changes in candidate selection. With 24 hours of later events, the 26-feature logistic regression improves mean reciprocal rank (MRR) by 0.039 on tgbl-wiki under exhaustive ranking, 0.046 under historical-random ranking, and 0.108 on tgbl-comment under historical-random ranking. The 26-feature logistic regression benefits from adding later features to past features, whereas replacing the past pair count with the later pair count reduces MRR by 0.157 on tgbl-wiki under exhaustive ranking. A control removing later interactions from query sources to historically observed destinations reduces the MRR gain by 59% on tgbl-wiki and 10% on tgbl-comment. Because this control removes more than repeated target pairs, these reductions cannot be attributed to pair recurrence alone. Later events improve the ranking of hidden interactions on both datasets, including tgbl-comment, where only 6.4% of target pairs reappeared within 24 hours.
