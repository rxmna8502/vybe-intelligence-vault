# Retrieval of Coastal Biogeochemical Parameters From Near-Surface Hyperspectral Remote Sensing Reflectance Using Physics-Aware Meta-Learning

## Summary

arXiv:2605.05623v4 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Hyperspectral in situ sensing has shown promise in retrieving aquatic water quality (WQ) parameters, such as total suspended solids (TSS), dissolved organic carbon (DOC), and total chlorophyll-a (TChl-a), for cost-effective management of coastal WQ. However, generalising such retrieval algorithms across water bodies remains challenging, as the relationship between remote sensing reflectance ($R_{rs}$) and WQ parameters can vary considerably from one region to another due to regional distinctions in environmental conditions and biogeochemistry that lead to different WQ ranges and bio-optical properties. In this study, we propose a two-stage physics-aware meta-learning framework for retrieving coastal WQ parameters from near-surface $R_{rs}$ observations. Unlike purely data-driven approaches trained solely on paired observations, the proposed framework incorporates physical knowledge through a two-stage learning process. In the first stage, a region-agnostic base model is pretrained using meta-learning on synthetic WQ--$R_{rs}$ samples generated by a bio-optical forward model parameterised with an in situ bio-optical spectral library. In the second stage, the pretrained model is fine-tuned using local samples to adapt to regional conditions. To evaluate the proposed approach, we collected in situ hyperspectral $R_{rs}$ and WQ measurements from five geographically distinct sites in Australian coastal waters.
