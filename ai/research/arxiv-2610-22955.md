# On the tradeoff between robustness and geometric accuracy in adversarial training

## Summary

arXiv:2610.22955v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: For non-parametric binary classification in the presence of an adversary, we prove an optimal quantitative estimate for adversarial classifiers controlling geometric accuracy, as measured by the Hausdorff distance to the Bayes classifier, in terms or their robustness, as encoded by the adversarial budget $\varepsilon$. This rigorously shows that robust classifiers sacrifice at most $O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})$ or $O(\varepsilon)$ in accuracy, depending on the regularity of the underlying Bayes classifier. In contrast to prior work on the incompatibility of robustness and accuracy over low-complexity hypothesis classes, our work shows that high-complexity hypothesis classes allow for robust classifiers with precisely quantified accuracy. Our results apply for adversaries determined by any norm on Euclidean space and in both the infinite and finite data settings.
