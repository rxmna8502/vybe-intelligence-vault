# Grounding Language Models in Speech Does Not Fix "AI Flavor": Calibrated LLM Judges, Goodhart Traps, and a Preference-Tuning Fix

## Summary

arXiv:2611.19840v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Chinese outputs of large language models carry a perceptible "AI flavor": formulaic sentence frames, stacked written-register connectives, repetition loops, and layout leakage. A popular hypothesis blames text-only training for the missing inner-speech layer of human writing; injecting speech or prosody signals should therefore reduce it. We test it with controlled interventions on a 0.6B model; all three fail: (i) phoneme-interleaved continued pretraining yields no spokenness gain under text-matched controls while raising repetition (+0.13 [+0.03, +0.23]); (ii) prosody-plan conditional generation reaches an adherence of only 0.257 against a pre-registered 0.50 gate; (iii) a rule-based reward built on TTS prosody-feature distance is not distinguishable in double-blind evaluation by either humans (8/16) or a calibrated LLM judge (9/16). A root cause is evaluation: single-rater blind evaluation at n~20 only detects extreme (>=85%) preferences. We therefore build a calibrated LLM-judge protocol: the judge passes catch trials on known-answer pairs (16/16; pilots 22/24 and 24/24, versus 0.75 for the human rater), then annotates with two independent judges under A/B swap, disagreement counted as a tie. Using the judge's agree labels directly as DPO pairs (no reward model), we obtain the first positive result: held-out blind win rate 0.833 (tie=0.5; bootstrap CI [0.708, 0.938]; hard-agree 0.900 [0.699, 0.972]), repetition 0.114 to 0.000, MMLU within noise. We further identify a Goodhart channel: the judge's length preference transduces into output-length collapse via pairwise DPO (median 75 chars vs a 300-char prompt); an SFT-anchor repairs it (w is a style knob: w=0.1 preserves gains). Scaling preference data from 89 to 240 pairs yields no additional judge-visible gain. We release RA-Eval v0.1: a three-layer Chinese AI-flavor evaluation framework with 382 judge-annotated preference pairs.
