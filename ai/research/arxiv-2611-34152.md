# Lost in the Grid: Where Multimodal LLMs Fail Between Seeing and Planning

## Summary

arXiv:2611.34152v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) excel at static visual understanding and are increasingly applied to spatial planning, where a model interprets a visual layout and outputs an action sequence to reach a goal. Grid games are a common testbed for this ability because their rules are explicit and a solver can verify every move. Yet existing benchmarks mostly report end-to-end success rates, which hide whether a failure stems from visual perception, rule application, or multi-step planning. We introduce GridProbe, a diagnostic benchmark that 1) locates the failing stage between visual perception and planning and 2) verifies the diagnosis by targeted fine-tuning. On Sokoban, GridProbe pairs end-to-end solving with four probes (recognition, localization, single-step prediction, and state tracking) and gives every board both as an image and as an equivalent text grid. Evaluating seven MLLMs on Sokoban and a maze game reveals distinct bottlenecks behind similar success rates: proprietary models are limited by multi-step planning, whereas open-source models are limited by rule application or by visual grid indexing, i.e., counting rows and columns. Planning failures also hide perception failures: when planning is easier, the visual cost of the same model grows from 7.6 to 37.7 points. The diagnosis identifies what to train: fine-tuning only the vision module of the perception-limited model on board reading raises its interactive Sokoban success 3.5-fold (5.8% to 20.4%) and transfers zero-shot to the unseen maze game (single-turn success 13.8% to 67.1%), whereas the same data does not help a rule-limited model and training on solver solutions brings no gain. We will release the benchmark and code at https://github.com/suat-llm/GridProbe.
