# LaMe: a Joint Latent Mediation Framework for High-Dimensional Systems

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18180v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Mediation analysis seeks to understand how an exposure influences an outcome through intermediate variables. However, as increasingly rich datasets become available, hundreds to thousands of potential mediators make these systems difficult to model and hard to interpret. Latent-variable approaches offer a natural way to accommodate these high-dimensional settings. However, existing latent mediation methods face limitations, including overparameterization, biased inference, or limited scalability. We develop LaMe, a joint linear-Gaussian framework that summarizes a high-dimensional exposure-mediator-outcome system through a small number of latent variables. We identify natural direct and indirect effects under an explicit assumption that the manifest measurements fully capture the latent mediators, and characterize conditions under which the latent representation effectively restricts the observed cross-covariance structure. Simulations assess recovery of observed association parameters, natural effects, and manifest features associated with latent mediation pathways across a range of challenging settings. Four real-world applications demonstrate competitive predictive performance with compact latent representations, including single-dimensional representations of biomarker and proteomic measurements. Together, these results establish LaMe as a practical framework for estimating and summarizing multivariate mediation systems under explicit structural and measurement assumptions.
