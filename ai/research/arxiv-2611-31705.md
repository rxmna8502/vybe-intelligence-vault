# FoveaWAM: Role-Aware 4D World-Action Modeling for Dynamic Manipulation

## Summary

arXiv:2611.31705v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: To manipulate a moving object, a policy must predict the scene at the moment its action executes, yet every millisecond spent predicting pushes that moment further away. Scene-level forecasts are computationally expensive, whereas target-centric forecasts can omit nearby objects that affect contact. Our key insight is that what a policy must predict decouples by role: only the target's pose, which determines contact, requires millimeter precision, while the surroundings matter primarily through the clearance they leave and the motion they impart. We present FoveaWAM, a native 4D world-action model that allocates precision according to these roles. Specifically, it models the target's past and future motion as a metric SE(3) trajectory, the surroundings as sparse 3D flow, and relies on the current frame for everything static. This compact representation lets FoveaWAM act in 15.1 ms on one RTX 5090, about 10 times faster than video world-action baselines, leaving substantially less time for the scene to evolve before action execution. To evaluate each policy under its own latency, we introduce OnTimeBench, a dynamic manipulation benchmark in which the scene keeps moving while the policy computes. FoveaWAM reaches 82.1% success on OnTimeBench, 20.8 points above the strongest baseline, and also leads on DOMINO and real-robot tasks. Project page: https://foveawam.github.io/
