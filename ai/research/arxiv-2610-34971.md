# SVD-Attention: Efficiently Analyzing Attention Blocks in Transformers

## Summary

arXiv:2610.34971v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Pretrained transformers spend a large share of their inference compute inside self-attention, yet the per-head query-key interaction is structurally low-rank by construction. We ask a diagnostic question: across layers and architectures, which heads actually use the full rank budget on real data, and which do not? We propose SVD-Attention, a post-hoc method that decomposes the per-head interaction matrix $M_h$ and scores every singular mode by both its spectral magnitude and its activation variance across tokens on a small calibration set. The score keeps the modes the head can amplify and that real data actually activates, and discards the rest; the resulting factorization is exact for the retained modes and needs no finetuning. Across seven pretrained models and five task types, the diagnostic finds a task-dependent pattern: redundancy sits in the early to middle layers for sentiment and image classification and for generation, and in the late layers for dense prediction and extractive question answering. It is also predictive: truncating the layers it flags costs at most $1.75$ F1, while truncating the same number of layers elsewhere in the same model costs up to $50$ F1. SVD-Attention is an analysis tool rather than a compression method: acting on it reduces inference FLOPs by $0.8$-$1.5\%$ on its own and by $8$-$11\%$ when composed with weight pruning, with $0$-$4\%$ change in task quality.
