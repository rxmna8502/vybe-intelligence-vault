# Partially Interpretable Decision Trees

## Summary

arXiv:2611.06428v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Partially interpretable models extend simple interpretable models with the option to defer complicated inputs to an accurate but uninterpretable black-box model. By deferring more inputs, the model gets more accurate; by deferring fewer, it gets more interpretable. Existing procedures to train such partially interpretable models build on linear models or decision rules. We propose three algorithms for decision trees: a partially interpretable extension of the existing Branches algorithm, which performs exact search to optimize the tree; a conversion algorithm that we run on top of standard CART, which is computationally more efficient; and a generalized CART whose splitting criterion takes into account the option to defer while growing the tree. Perhaps surprisingly, the performance of the three algorithms is similar across many realworld datasets. We can explain this through a formal bound on the potential gain of smart tree construction, provided the black box accuracy is sufficiently homogeneous across the domain. We also construct a synthetic dataset where the accuracy of the black box is deliberately inhomogeneous. There, our deferral-aware generalization of CART does outperform regular CART and matches the performance of exact search. Finally, we evaluate an application to concept bottleneck models. Partially interpretable decision trees can replace uninterpretable neural networks after the bottleneck. On undeferred inputs, the model is fully interpretable, and deferring preserves accuracy.
