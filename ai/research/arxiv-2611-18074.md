# Learning nonlinear interactions and cross-feature couplings with product-unit residual networks

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18074v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Understanding nonlinear feature interactions is crucial in science and engineering, yet standard multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) often capture such interactions only implicitly, leading to entangled representations that can impair robustness and interpretability. We investigate complex-valued product-unit residual networks (CV-PURe) that integrate multiplicative product units with residual connections to explicitly model cross-feature couplings while stabilizing optimization. We conduct a systematic evaluation on an interaction-driven synthetic benchmark and real-world datasets and evaluate prediction together with input-level interaction recovery under a reproducible protocol. Across five data splits and five training seeds, CV-PURe achieves the lowest mean RMSE on four of six scientific benchmarks, reducing Friedman error by 53.9% relative to MLP. Its Friedman interaction maps assign 60.7% of total strength to the known interacting pair, compared with 31.0% for MLP. On a synthetic task containing third- and fourth-order products and 20 distractors, CV-PURe reduces RMSE from 0.1376 to 0.0797 relative to a complex-valued MLP and recovers all nine true pairwise projections in every training/sampling repetition. Bike Sharing experiments connect improved prediction to the contribution of temporal, calendar, and seasonal context through paired ablations of both PURe variants. Parkinsons Telemonitoring extends the context analysis to repeated measurements. Noise and learning-curve evaluations demonstrate sustained predictive gains under perturbed inputs and limited supervision, while inference measurements quantify computational cost. This article extends our ICCS 2026 paper, Modeling Nonlinear Feature Interactions with Product-Unit Residual Networks (doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29921-5_6).
