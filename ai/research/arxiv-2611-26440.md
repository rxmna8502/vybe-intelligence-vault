# Learning to Defer under Expert Drift

## Summary

arXiv:2611.26440v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: The learning to defer (L2D) framework enables safety in machine learning and AI systems by allocating difficult or critical decisions to a human expert. However, most prior work on L2D assumes fixed expert reliability, overlooking temporal fluctuations in human performance due to factors such as fatigue and cognitive biases. Humans commonly exhibit such substantial, systematic performance shifts across a wide range of domains, from high-stakes settings such as radiology, aviation, and judicial decision-making, to everyday tasks such as crowdsourced image annotation. We propose Expert-Drift-Adapted Learning-to-Defer (EDA-L2D), a novel expert-aware L2D framework that explicitly models these temporal shifts by conditioning the deferral head on recent histories of expert outcomes via a sequence model. This history-aware approach enables adaptive deferral policies that anticipate reliability fluctuations and better allocate decisions between human and machine over time. We provide comprehensive experiments across three diverse tasks -- image classification, medical diagnosis, and hate speech detection -- including generalization analyses under realistic deferral conditions and irregular shifts in expert reliability. Our results show that EDA-L2D adapts more effectively than prior L2D methods to diverse temporal patterns of expert reliability, resulting in improved system performance and more effective deferral decisions.
