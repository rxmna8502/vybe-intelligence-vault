# Can LLMs Understand What They Predict? Evaluating Latent Causal Structure Inference in Financial Markets

## Summary

arXiv:2611.23533v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly evaluated through their ability to predict future outcomes, implicitly assuming that predictive success reflects an understanding of the underlying system. However, accurate predictions can often be achieved without recovering the latent mechanisms that generate observable outcomes. To examine this assumption, we introduce LUCID, a benchmark that evaluates whether models can infer latent market structures from event descriptions and historical financial observations. Models generate event-induced causal graphs representing inferred market structure and are evaluated through a reconstruction-based framework. Our evaluation of 6 open-weight and proprietary LLMs shows that adding their generated graphs generally reduces reconstruction quality compared with using historical observations alone, although a few metrics improve. These findings suggest that inferring informative, event-specific market structures from observable evidence remains challenging for the evaluated LLMs.
