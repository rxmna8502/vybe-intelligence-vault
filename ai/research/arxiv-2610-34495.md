# Approximate Unlearning in Prototype-based-classifiers

## Summary

arXiv:2610.34495v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: The recent data and AI regulations and governance needs have highlighted the need for removing specific data even from models trained on or with them. While retraining the entire model is a theoretically trivial solution, it is computationally expensive, thereby motivating research in Machine Unlearning. This paper introduces a robust approximate unlearning technique for the family of prototype-based classifiers called Generalized Learning Vector Quantization, aimed at removal of effects of specified samples, either due to deletion request or due to them being identified as noisy or corrupt. We first show the impact of the different sizes of the forget set, as well as the characteristics of the data in this set, on the unlearned model. To address the sources of instabilities detected in this unlearning method we propose an adapted version of this technique, such that the unlearning is robust to the characteristics of the forget set. Thereafter using the intrinsic interpretability property of GLVQ and appropriate metrics defined in unlearning literature, we show the efficacy (approximately 0 deviation of parameters and performance of unlearned model relative to retrained model) and efficiency (6X for smallest dataset to 10^(5)X in the largest dataset) of the proposed unlearning techniques compared to naive retrained, on six benchmark datasets for tabular data recommended by literature. Although in this work we investigated only binary class problems, the proposed robust unlearning strategy can be readily applied for removing sample effects from any trained GLVQ model, i.e., it does not require any modification in the training scheme of GLVQ.
