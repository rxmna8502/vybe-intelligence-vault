# Morpho-Spectral Projection Analysis (MSPA) for Plant Spectroscopy

## Summary

arXiv:2611.24599v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Plant reflectance spectroscopy across the visible, near-infrared, and shortwave infrared domain encodes foliar biochemical and anatomical traits into contiguous spectral signatures with locally differentiable structure; however, extreme inter-band collinearity and unfavorable sample-to-feature ratios challenge predictive modeling. Conventional dimensionality reduction methods routinely adopted in spectroscopy, are column-permutation invariant: they treat adjacent wavelengths as unordered tabular columns and discard the sequential differential geometry of absorption features. Here, we propose Morpho-Spectral Projection Analysis (MSPA), a supervised dimensionality reduction operator that projects contiguous spectra into a compact morpho-spectral subspace through three complementary blocks: (i) additive continuum-removed absorption depth, area, and red-edge inflection kinematics; (ii) local multi-order singular eigenmodes (0th, 1st, and 2nd derivatives) partitioned across contiguous radiative sub-windows; and (iii) global Fisher-weighted canonical subspaces regularized via trace-normalized isotropic covariance shrinkage. We validated MSPA across 8,963 spectra spanning five plant systems, coupled with six classical machine learning classifiers and four Tabular Foundation Models (TabPFN v3, TabICL v2, TabDPT v1.2, and TabFM). Despite compressing the 1,580-band spectrum by 96%, MSPA outperformed the full spectrum in 92.0% of all benchmarks and conventional reducers in 90.0% of all model-crop evaluations, raising the global mean macro-F1 from 0.7756 (full spectrum) to 0.8520, accelerating in-context inference by up to 126.5x. Designed as a self-contained spectral projection operator, the MSPA method can be directly integrated into spectroscopy workflows via the open-source code available at GitHub: https://github.com/Lucas-Osco/mspa.
