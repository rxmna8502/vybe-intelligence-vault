# State Space Models Naturally Produce Time Cell and Oscillatory Behaviors and Scale to Abstract Cognitive Functions

## Summary

arXiv:2507.13638v4 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: A grand challenge in modern neuroscience is bridging the gap between the microscale neural circuits and cognitive functions. While extensive knowledge exists about neuronal connectivity and biophysics, how low-level phenomena give rise to abstract behaviors remains largely unresolved. Here, we propose that a mathematical framework based on State Space Models offers mathematical sufficiency and computational plausibility to generate response profiles that resemble time cells, ramping activity, and oscillatory responses. Our results suggest that such behaviors can emerge from a structured multi-timescale complex rotational basis, which enables optimized history compression and naturally generates structured temporal dynamics even prior to training, mirroring recent findings in biological circuits. Learning exploits and adapts to the pre-existing temporal basis for specific tasks. We show that the model can readily scale to abstract cognitive functions such as event counting, supporting its use as a computationally tractable framework for understanding neural activities.
