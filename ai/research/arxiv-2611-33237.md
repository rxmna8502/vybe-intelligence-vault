# Resource-Efficient Multimodal Fusion for Early Rice Blast Forecasting in Sustainable Agriculture

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33237v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Resource-efficient artificial intelligence has become increasingly important for sustainable agriculture, where timely disease forecasting must be achieved under computational and energy constraints. Early estimation of rice blast can enhance food security by optimizing crop management practices while reducing excessive pesticide use. However, existing frameworks often suffer from high computational overhead and limited capability to model the complex spectral-temporal dependencies present in heterogeneous meteorological and remote sensing data. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a lightweight multimodal fusion framework comprising an attentive positional encoding-based Transformer and a convolutional neural network (CNN)-embedded gated recurrent unit (GRU) for efficient spectral-temporal representation learning. The attention-enhanced positional encoding assigns adaptive importance to sequential meteorological tokens, enabling effective modelling of temporal characteristics. In parallel, the lightweight CNN extracts discriminative spectral features, while the GRU captures temporal dynamics from remote sensing imagery acquired over two time-instances. A multimodal fusion layer integrates these heterogeneous representations to forecast rice blast risk 5-10 days before symptom manifestation across diverse agro-climatic zones. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving a validation accuracy of 96.93% and an F1-score of 95.79%. In addition to its predictive performance, the lightweight multimodal architecture substantially reduces computational complexity, inference latency, and carbon emissions, making it an energy-efficient solution for large-scale disease forecasting. The proposed framework provides a practical foundation for resource-efficient artificial intelligence in sustainable agriculture.
