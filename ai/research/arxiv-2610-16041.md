# Autoregressive Decoding as a Martingale: Characterizing the Stochastic Dynamics of LLM Generation

## Summary

arXiv:2610.16041v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable success across a wide range of language tasks. At their core, autoregressive LLMs are conditional probability models that learn to approximate the next-token distribution given the preceding context. Despite this probabilistic nature, the stochastic dynamics of autoregressive decoding have rarely been investigated from a rigorous probabilistic perspective. In this work, we define the per-step prediction error as the difference between the sampled token indicator and the model's conditional predictive distribution, and prove that the cumulative error process is a martingale with respect to the decoding filtration, implying that its conditional first-order moment is identically zero. Beyond this first-order property, we derive a closed-form expression for the second-order behavior of the martingale sequence: its predictable quadratic variation reduces to $\sum_t\left(1-p(y_t\mid y_{<t})\right)$, computable from realized token probabilities alone. This identity establishes a direct theoretical connection between sequence fluctuations and predictive uncertainty during autoregressive decoding. White-box experiments on Llama-3.x and Qwen2.5 families (1B to 72B parameters) verify that the empirically measured second-order moments closely match the theoretical predictions derived from our martingale analysis. These results provide a probabilistic framework for analyzing autoregressive LLM decoding, reveal intrinsic probabilistic fingerprints of different language models, and establish a rigorous mathematical foundation for LLM-generated text detection and uncertainty quantification.
