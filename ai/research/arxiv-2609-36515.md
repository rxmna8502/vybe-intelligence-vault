# Large-scale factor analysis shows machine intelligence is only partially interpretable

## Summary

arXiv:2609.36515v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: A common assumption in language model development is that cognitive abilities are organized around a general, domain-free intelligence factor, like fluid intelligence in humans. This assumption is rarely tested directly, and prior attempts have done so only at a much smaller scale. We take a latent variable approach to intelligence in language models, similar to how psychometricians study psychological constructs. Using factor analysis, we analyzed 13,230 published evaluation scores covering 1,618 language models across 456 text-only benchmarks. Because the data are sparse, we triangulate our analysis across different data densifiers and imputation methods. Across the imputations that pass our quality filter, the general factor's share of variance in the summed score ($\omega_h$) ranges from 0.04 to 0.76, and its share of the common variance (ECV) from 0.11 to 0.52. A robust pattern across different modes of bias is that 1. same-subject benchmarks are not guaranteed to place closely in vector space, with weak median label cohesion, 2. benchmarks loading highest on $G$ tend to be miscellaneous rather than thematically coherent, and 3. standard "intelligence" benchmarks do not dominate the share of ability variance the general factor explains, and there is a lack of evidence that $G$ is well-proxied by them. We hypothesize that targeting a single conceptual ability has little support, since the first-order abilities it would have to reach are often partially idiosyncratic and not identifiable in practice. This would mean that, from public benchmark scores alone, general intelligence is hard to define, identify, and target as a single construct in language model development. Future experiments should run training regimes that target both theoretically abstract general ability and tasks with semantic similarity and examine how abilities transfer across test sets of diverse formats.
