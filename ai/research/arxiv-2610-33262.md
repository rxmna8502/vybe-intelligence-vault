# Not All Mislabels Are Equal: Efficient Task-Aware Auditing with Confidence Sequences

## Summary

arXiv:2610.33262v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: We study efficient, adaptive auditing of categorical labels in a fixed dataset, motivated by biodiversity credit programs in which expert verification of species records is costly and downstream scores (e.g., payment) are more sensitive to errors on certain labels than on others. Our proposed ATLAS framework couples anytime-valid inference with a task-aware adaptive auditing policy. Representing label corrections through a confusion matrix, we prioritize which data points to inspect next based on both label uncertainty and the influence of potential label corrections on the downstream score function. In our main case study on Kenyan biodiversity data, task-aware auditing reduces the expert review needed to estimate both the mislabel rate and the nonlinear biodiversity score while maintaining anytime-valid guarantees. We also evaluate ATLAS on public datasets for image classification and named-entity recognition. Across our experiments with linear and nonlinear scores, ATLAS generally reaches the same precision as competing anytime-valid methods with substantially fewer audits. When distribution-free guarantees are prohibitively costly, we develop a model-based Bayesian alternative that can further reduce the number of audits by relying on modeling assumptions. Our biodiversity case study highlights a gap between statistical validity and practical feasibility: generic methods such as Serfling's bound and existing confidence-sequence procedures are rigorous but can require prohibitively many expert reviews in practice. Together, these results show task-aware auditing can significantly reduce the number of expert audits needed while providing finite-sample, anytime-valid guarantees
