# A Horizon-Specific Attribute-Conditioned Neural Network for Estimating Health-State Transition Probabilities in Irregular Longitudinal Data: The LANTERN Framework

## Summary

arXiv:2606.13880v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Accurate estimation of health-state transition probabilities is essential for long-term care insurance modeling. Conventional actuarial multi-state models provide an interpretable framework but may be restrictive in capturing nonlinear relationships and accumulated health histories from irregular longitudinal observations. This paper proposes LANTERN, a history-dependent neural framework that integrates recurrent health representations, elapsed observation time, and adaptive demographic and socioeconomic attribute conditioning to estimate horizon-specific transition probabilities among Healthy, Mild disability, Severe disability, and Death states. Using longitudinal Health and Retirement Study data, we evaluate our model against a recurrent neural network, multinomial logistic regression, gradient-boosted trees, and an actuarial continuous-time Markov chain across one-, two-, and three-year forecast horizons. At the primary two-year horizon, LANTERN achieves competitive predictive accuracy and calibration, with improvements over selected benchmarks. Its aggregated transition-matrix estimates outperform multinomial logistic regression and are comparable to those of the strongest remaining benchmarks. These findings suggest that history-conditioned neural estimation provides a flexible and competitive approach to estimating horizon-specific health-state transition probabilities, with potential applications in actuarial long-term care modeling.
