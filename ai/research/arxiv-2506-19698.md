# Data-Driven Maintenance Optimization: Aligning Prognostic Learning with Decision Objective

## Summary

arXiv:2506.19698v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Estimation error in machine learning prognostic models can propagate into poor maintenance decisions. We study a data-driven predictive maintenance problem coupling health-condition estimation with a joint ordering-replacement model. We show that the conventional Estimate-Then-Optimize (ETO) approach yields inconsistent, suboptimal decisions for a broad class of such problems. To bridge the gap, we propose an Integrated Estimate-Optimize (IEO) framework that directly targets maintenance cost as the prognostic learning objective, bypassing surrogate statistical accuracy metrics such as Kullback--Leibler divergence or mean squared error. Theoretically, we prove out-of-sample performance guarantees of the IEO framework under certain conditions, including i.i.d. and sliding-window data-generating processes. Computationally, we develop a training procedure combining Monte Carlo dropout, central-difference gradient approximation, and an ETO warm-start to handle discrete maintenance decisions and nonlinear costs. Experiments on the four CMAPSS subsets demonstrate that, under the nominal setting, IEO reduces mean maintenance cost by 14.8--22.7% relative to ETO and by 7.2--11.8% relative to a decision-oriented baseline, while also reducing failure rates and cost variation. These improvements are obtained despite higher prediction errors. Increasing the ratio of unplanned-downtime costs to planned-downtime costs makes this tradeoff more pronounced: IEO shifts predictions further toward RUL underestimation, accompanied by greater cost savings and fewer failures. These findings support aligning prognostic learning with maintenance objectives when imperfect predictions must guide decisions under costly unplanned downtime.
