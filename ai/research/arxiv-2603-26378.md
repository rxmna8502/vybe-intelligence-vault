# Generative machine learning for protein design

## Summary

arXiv:2603.26378v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Designing proteins with specific biological functions is of considerable interest for both the development of new therapeutics and the engineering of enzymes, yet achieving the intended activity remains challenging. Generative artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a powerful tool to explore the vast space of possible designs by learning from protein sequence and structural data. Here, we review 59 generative approaches across six protein design tasks, including sequence generation, backbone generation, structure-conditioned sequence design, joint sequence-structure design, motif scaffolding, and binder and antibody design. We outline common generative approaches and protein representations and discuss how structural constraints and functional information are utilized during generation. This is followed by a discussion of the computational and experimental approaches used to evaluate designed proteins and what such assessments reveal about their properties. Finally, we discuss current limitations in designing proteins beyond well-represented families and incorporating experimental feedback into the design process. We expect that rapid advances in generative modeling will enable more reliable design of a wider range of protein structures and functions over the coming years.
