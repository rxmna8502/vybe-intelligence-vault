# Amortized Inference of Multi-Modal Posteriors using Likelihood-Weighted Normalizing Flows

## Summary

arXiv:2512.04954v4 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: We present a technique for posterior estimation using Normalizing Flows trained with likelihood-weighted importance sampling, the special case of neural importance sampling in which the proposal is the prior. This approach allows for the efficient inference of theoretical parameters in high-dimensional inverse problems without the need for posterior training samples. We implement the method on multi-modal benchmark tasks in 2D and 3D to check for the efficacy. A critical observation of our study is the impact of the base distribution on the modelled posteriors. We find that standard unimodal base distributions leave spurious probability \textit{bridges} between well-separated modes. We demonstrate that initializing the flow with a Gaussian Mixture Model that matches the number of target modes significantly improves reconstruction fidelity, as measured by some distance and divergence metrics. Finally, we apply this method to a curated problem in heavy flavour physics --- the extraction of the Wolfenstein parameters from the CP asymmetry in $B^0\to J/\psi\,K^0$; it is multimodal, non-Gaussian, and asymmetric in its mode weights --- and compare the results against a well-converged Markov Chain Monte Carlo reference and an exact reference computed by quadrature, using different metrics. Used as a proposal for importance sampling, the trained flow removes its remaining bias and gives the evidence.
