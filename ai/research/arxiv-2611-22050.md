# DSADiff: Data Structure-Aware Diffusion for Crystal Structure Prediction via Mitigating Complexity- and Imbalance-Induced Biases

## Summary

arXiv:2611.22050v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP) is an important step in accelerating the generation and discovery of new materials. In recent years, diffusion-based CSP methods have made significant progress. However, existing models still suffer from generation bias when facing diverse materials spaces, while systematic approaches for tracing the sources of such performance variations remain limited. To address this issue, we propose LAS (Layered Analysis Strategy), a mechanism analysis framework that identifies crystal complexity and elemental composition imbalance as two key factors affecting generation difficulty and further reveals their relationships with model generation performance. Building on these findings, we further propose DSADiff (Data Structure-Aware Diffusion), a data structure-aware diffusion model that transforms the key factors identified by LAS into a unified generation difficulty representation and uses it to jointly drive feature modeling and training strategy optimization. This enables the model to adapt its representation and generation capability to samples with different data structures and difficulty levels, thereby mitigating learning bias across samples. Experiments on three CSP benchmark datasets, including Carbon-24, demonstrate that DSADiff consistently improves both Match Rate and RMSE, with a maximum Match Rate improvement of 9%, surpassing existing state-of-the-art methods and achieving new state-of-the-art performance. This work deepens the understanding of diffusion-based CSP models from the perspective of generation bias and provides a new direction for designing less biased generative models for complex materials spaces.
