# Learned Network Dynamics for Model Predictive Traffic Signal Control under Incidents

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27435v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Model predictive control (MPC) of urban traffic signals relies on a model of how queues respond to signal decisions, which is hard to specify and calibrate in heterogeneous networks, particularly when incidents change capacities and turning shares. Measurements add a second limitation: the controlled lanes at the stop line are often short, so queues extending upstream of them fall outside lane-level measurements. We propose GNN-MPC, a receding-horizon controller that plans with action-conditioned network dynamics learned by graph neural networks. Phase sequences are coordinated across intersections by approximate best responses, with an acceptance test that keeps the predicted cost no higher than that of the initial plan. In microscopic simulation under non-recurrent incidents, GNN-MPC reduces travel time and increases completed trips relative to all tested controllers, on a homogeneous grid and a heterogeneous 21-intersection network. Controlled comparisons on the heterogeneous network distinguish the roles of observation and prediction. With nearly as many vehicles entering the network, GNN-MPC reduces travel time by 13% relative to MaxPressure with the same upstream measurement, and GNN-MPC without the upstream representation does not reproduce its performance. Under the same traffic measurements, planner and objective, replacing the learned dynamics with calibrated store-and-forward predictors increases travel time by 31-34%. Observing hidden demand and predicting its response to control are thus complementary requirements of network-level predictive control.
