# Enrichment-Oriented Virtual Screening of Perovskite Solar Cells through Reinforcement Learning

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18043v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Experimental optimization of perovskite solar cells is constrained by combinatorial diversity in absorber compositions, device layers, and processing, which makes virtual screening attractive for prioritizing recipes before fabrication. We develop a surrogate-guided reinforcement-learning framework that casts recipe generation as virtual screening, enriching candidate shortlists near high-performing experimental neighbors. A frozen Tabular Prior-data Fitted Network (TabPFN) guides recipe generation, while a reference pool of 890 measured devices enables neighborhood-based assessment. Five-fold evaluation on 1,846 devices shows top-ten selection raises mean power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 19.68\% to 23.74\%. Three learned policies are compared with six baselines under 3,000 surrogate evaluations per run. In top-100 shortlists, Proximal policy optimization (PPO) and recurrent PPO (RPPO) achieve neighborhood-supported hit rates of 23\% and 24\% (7.9- and 8.3-fold Pure Random's 2.9\%), versus 10\% (3.4-fold) for the baselines with the highest supported hit rate (L-SHADE and Ax BO, tied) at $K = 100$. Gains coexist with weak-support fractions. Of 9,000 recipes, 65.4\% match a composition family at similarity 0.80, falling to 11.7\% at 0.90. Results support enrichment-oriented prioritization but not experimental performance, seed robustness, or matched-budget advantage.
