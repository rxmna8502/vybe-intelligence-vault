# Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18372v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapt web-pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions, so that web knowledge carries over to physical control. In under four years the field has grown from RT-2, the first VLA, to open models that fine-tune on a single consumer GPU and proprietary generalists that clean unseen homes. VLA research joins robotics, computer vision and language modelling, so this survey is written for researchers and practitioners from each of these fields. It reviews 89 works from December 2022 to October 2026, mainly on manipulation and navigation, plus humanoid control, world-action models and evaluation. Seven design axes follow a VLA's life cycle: model design (inputs and memory, architecture, action output, intermediate predictions), training (pretraining data, post-training) and deployment (real-time execution). We compare 22 models on these axes, with their training data, model size, inference speed and hardware. Three trends emerge. (i) Training data has broadened beyond teleoperated robot data (about 10,000 hours for $\pi_0$) to community-collected episodes and egocentric human video (20,000 hours for GR00T N1.7). (ii) Model sizes have split into two groups: most recent models have 3-7 billion parameters; a new group under one billion runs on consumer GPUs. (iii) Generalist VLAs handling both manipulation and navigation are emerging, such as UniVLA and Qwen-VLA. We also compare VLAs with world-action models, which predict future observations as well as actions, and review evidence that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores fail after small scene changes. A practitioner's guide gives a default for each design decision, and a glossary supports readers new to the field.
