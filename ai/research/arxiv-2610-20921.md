# A Multi-Tiered AI Accessibility Model (MAIAM): Proportionate Access and Capability Limits for Comprehensive Frontier AI Safety

## Summary

arXiv:2610.20921v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Most debate about releasing capable AI systems treats the choice as binary: keep the model behind an API, or publish the weights. That framing runs together two separate decisions. One is how capable a system may become. The other is who may obtain a given capability, and on what terms. This paper is about the second decision. We propose a Multi-Tiered AI Accessibility Model (MAIAM) that sorts access by purpose, safety, and capability instead of by release artifact. Tier 1, AI for Learning, is broadly accessible and deliberately capability-bounded. Tier 2, AI for Exploration, serves research and development under registration, identity, and usage controls that tighten as capability rises. Tier 3, Full-Scale Frontier AI, is available only through structured, recorded, and accountable access. The principle behind the tiers is that accessibility should be proportional to capability, safety, and risk. We show why publishing frontier-class weights forfeits every safety lever a developer has after release, and, using evidence that safety fine-tuning can be stripped from open weights for a few hundred dollars, why an evaluation of the base model says little about its derivatives. We give criteria for assigning a system to a tier along capability, autonomy, domain-consequence, and usage/safety axes, and combine MAIAM with the SAFE AI Operating Point into a two-part framework: a Capability Boundary that fixes the safety limits not to be crossed, and an Accessibility Boundary that fixes who may obtain a capability below that limit. Capability limits alone do not make AI safe; a tiered access regime administered by regulating bodies is also needed.
