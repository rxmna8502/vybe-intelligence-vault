# Reconstruction differences rival individual differences in the fly compass connectome and break trained circuit models

## Summary

arXiv:2610.29205v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Neural network models are increasingly constructed from synapse-resolution connectomes, and the same circuit is now available in reconstructions from other animals and pipelines. We do not know whether such models are dependent on the reconstruction they are built from, and whether connectome differences are biological or measurement errors. We studied the Drosophila head-direction circuit in nine versions: four flies (male CNS, hemibrain, FlyWire, BANC) and five variants of the same fly varying only by thresholds, proofreading or synapse detection. Using wiring-based correspondences validated by anatomical labels, we place all differences on one scale. A change of synapse detection within one fly changed the circuit more than halfway to the smallest between-fly difference on four distance measures; proofreading differences were smaller but depended on the metric, and left and right halves of one fly differed no more than random halves. Networks trained on each wiring to hold a cued heading developed near-perfect ring attractors, but their error increased from below 1 degree to 10-28 degrees when all weights changed by 1%. Training on each wiring did not transfer to any other connectome, including other versions of the same fly; refitting thirty cell-type gains, we repaired filtering-level differences but remaining error depended on wiring distance. Training with noise lowered precision and gave only partial transfer. In imaging data, the fly compass drifted gradually in darkness. Connectome-constrained models should report the reconstruction they were built on and be tested for robustness across reconstructions.
