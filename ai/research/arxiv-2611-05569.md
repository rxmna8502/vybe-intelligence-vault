# Role-based Effectiveness of an Epistemic Uncertainty Estimate in Training Models for Semantic Segmentation of 3D LiDAR Point Clouds

## Summary

arXiv:2611.05569v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Point cloud segmentation is a fundamental task in 3D data analysis, with applications across robotics, augmented and virtual reality, and remote sensing. However, the inherent sparsity, noise, and unordered nature of point clouds introduce significant challenges, particularly in achieving reliable classification. While the state-of-the-art rely on training models for explicit modeling of uncertainty, there is a gap in exploring different roles that estimated uncertainty using generative approaches can play. This work investigates the impact of deterministic uncertainty in point cloud segmentation by explicitly modeling epistemic uncertainty and using it in different roles, namely, as a feature and in loss function. We evaluate a baseline artificial neural network (ANN) and two uncertainty-aware variants (uncertainty as an additional input feature and as a per-sample confidence weight in the training loss) on four large-scale LiDAR benchmarks spanning airborne and mobile acquisition, namely ISPRS Vaihingen, AHN3, Paris-Lille-3D, and Toronto-3D, together with PointNet++ and RandLA-Net references and their uncertainty-augmented counterparts under canonical spatially disjoint train and test splits. Under this protocol, uncertainty-aware modeling improves mean Intersection over Union, recovers minority classes that the baseline ANN never predicts at all, and in half of the configurations improves the alignment between prediction error and estimated uncertainty as measured by Patch Accuracy versus Patch Uncertainty. Our findings indicate that uncertainty can play multiple roles in the segmentation pipeline rather than as a device for improving accuracy alone.
