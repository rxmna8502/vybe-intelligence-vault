# Reinforcement Learning Can Be Recovered From Its Own Rollouts

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33102v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Broadly speaking, reinforcement learning (RL) post-training converts compute into model capabilities by training on model-generated data. Typically, the rollouts are discarded after each update, and the knowledge acquired during RL resides in the model weights. In this work, we investigate how much of this knowledge is retained in the rollouts themselves. A natural first attempt is to replay the original RL algorithm on archived (rather than freshly generated) rollouts, but this generally leads to catastrophic collapse. We provide a simple explanation for this phenomenon, which in turn suggests a remedy we call maximum likelihood replay: fitting the model to its archived rollouts. Surprisingly, starting from the same base model and replaying the rollouts in the order in which they were generated, without any distinction between correct and incorrect responses, is sufficient to recover the performance of online RL. In fact, a single maximum likelihood update per rollout batch closely tracks the entire learning curve of online RL. Furthermore, maximum likelihood replay on the rollouts of the last few RL steps often suffices to recover the final model's performance. This suggests that much of the compute invested in RL goes into discovering an improved sampling distribution rather than learning it from samples.
