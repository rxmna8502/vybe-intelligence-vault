# On Grokking and the Dynamics of Generalization

## Summary

arXiv:2611.33825v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: We extend the signal-channel theory of grokking introduced by Litman and Guo by relating training dissipation and evaluation transfer to population risk. For preconditioned gradient flows with time-dependent kernels, we distinguish two sources of limited evaluation visibility, namely small cumulative dissipation and weak transfer. We obtain quantitative bounds for task visibility and establish their stability under signal filtering and changes of evaluation distribution. We describe how task directions enter the effective signal channel through spectral activation or transport. We establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the transferred signal to decrease squared population risk and derive classification error bounds under margin assumptions. These results yield sufficient conditions for the same training trajectory to generalize under one evaluation distribution while failing under another. We show that differences in spectral learning rates can produce arbitrarily long delays between low training loss and low population risk. By relating classification accuracy to the distribution of margin crossing times, we construct a family of gradient flows in which the generalization delay tends to infinity, while both the duration of the transition from zero to full accuracy and the corresponding variation of population risk tend to zero. These phenomena occur without feature evolution during training. Finally, we prove that the occurrence and timing of generalization cannot generally be inferred from training dynamics alone, but depend on the geometry of evaluation transfer and its relation to the target.
