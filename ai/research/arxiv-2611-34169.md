# Multi-view fnirs identifies neurophysiological subtypes in post-stroke aphasia

## Summary

arXiv:2611.34169v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Post-stroke aphasia is clinically heterogeneous, and conventional classifications may not capture differences in brain functional organisation. We tested whether resting-state and task-based functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) could identify neurophysiological subgroups alongside clinical assessment.Seventy-nine patients underwent unsupervised clustering using three fNIRS feature views: resting-state activity and connectivity, language-task activation, and rest-to-task connectivity reconfiguration. Similarity network fusion and spectral clustering integrated the views. Clinical and behavioural measures were excluded from clustering.Three clusters (n = 32, 17 and 30) were identified. This solution had the largest eigengap and was the only candidate significant after permutation testing and Holm correction (adjusted P = 0.001). It was stable under subsampling (mean adjusted Rand index, 0.773), but agreement fell to 0.217 when the resting-state view was removed. Clusters differed in most regional intrinsic-activity measures and all resting-state connectivity summaries, with few differences in task activation or connectivity reconfiguration. Their profiles showed higher right anterior prefrontal activity, distributed fronto-sensorimotor and parietal alterations, and reduced interhemispheric connectivity, respectively.Cluster membership was not significantly associated with conventional aphasia subtype (Cram\'er's V = 0.287), and baseline clinical language scores and task performance did not differ between clusters.These findings identify three neurophysiological subgroups distinguished mainly by intrinsic activity and resting-state functional connectivity, not clearly aligned with conventional aphasia classifications or baseline clinical measures. Multi-view fNIRS analysis may complement clinical characterisation, requiring independent and longitudinal validation.
