# Rethinking Cross-Sensor Domain Adaptation for Bridge Point Cloud Semantic Segmentation

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27060v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Segmentation models for bridge point clouds lose accuracy when trained on terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) data and applied to mobile laser scanning (MLS) data. This study re-examines the SemanticBridge TLS-to-MLS protocol to determine how sampling density, surface roughness, color, and acquisition viewpoint are expressed in the 8-cm sparse-voxel representation, and how adaptation gains depend on source-model convergence. Using one sparse-convolution U-Net, we combine raw-point statistics with controlled input perturbations and compare adaptive batch normalization, entropy minimization, Mean Teacher, and confidence-filtered self-training under 100- and 300-epoch schedules. TLS and MLS have similar nearest-neighbor spacing after preprocessing (12.08 and 12.02 mm), and a 4-mm perturbation of TLS points changes voxel occupancy by only 4.4%. Color distributions differ systematically, but zeroing color on identical voxels changes target mean intersection over union (mIoU) by only +0.02, and by +0.06 after the color augmentation is scale-corrected. The sensors also observe different portions of the bridge, yielding different class proportions. A 300-epoch source-only model obtains 57.80 mIoU on the paired-bridge protocol. Confidence-filtered self-training improves the 100-epoch baseline, but from a converged model it changes target mIoU by -0.97 (source-selected checkpoints) and -2.44 (final-50-epoch means). Repeated evaluation (standard deviation 0.27-0.64) and seed variation (1.88 across three seeds) are comparable to several method differences. Adaptation results on this benchmark therefore depend on representation scale, source-model convergence, and evaluation variability.
