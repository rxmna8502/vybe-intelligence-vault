# PF-Exec: Large-Scale UAV Relay Load Balancing with an Anchored Potential Direction Decider and a Per-Dimension Gain-Learning Executor

## Summary

arXiv:2611.17492v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Relay load balancing in large-scale UAV networks requires joint decisions on association, bandwidth, power and hovering position, while the dimension grows with the number of UAVs and each UAV sees only its local state. This paper splits the decision into two layers. An anchored potential direction decider emits a closed-form target at every instant and returns its direction only. A gain-learning executor maps a local observation into a positive gain per dimension, composes gain and target, and clips the action against the physical single-step bound; the two layers form PF-Exec. The unit gain is the default start, so the executor reduces to the pure potential policy before training. The load is first-order homogeneous in bandwidth, making the bandwidth target closed form, and the position target is the demand-weighted centroid of the served users. The system uses a two-hop access and millimetre-wave backhaul link with soft association, a 3GPP UMa-AV channel with two shadowing layers, an OU-type social-force crowd and a quadrotor physical layer. Evaluation spans all fleet sizes, two crowd scenarios, independent seeds, the published baselines in their own settings and a degraded-information line. In the highest-pressure burst setting the fixed-step comparison breaks down on part of the seeds, losing fairness and mean load at once, while PF-Exec holds fairness near one. Among the ten comparison methods only the greedy heuristic survives that setting; its load variance stays far higher. Under stale global state and position noise the fairness index stays high at both largest-scale scenarios. Flight energy is nearly identical between the learned and the fixed-step executor, and three constants are fixed once across all settings. The convergence properties of both layers, from the truncated gain iteration to the perturbed tracking bound, are proved in the companion analysis.
