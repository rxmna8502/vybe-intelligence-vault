# An Automated Framework Integrating Deep Learning and Anatomical Reasoning for Cardiac Phenotyping From CCTA

## Summary

arXiv:2611.32114v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) depicts coronary anatomy and cardiac fat, but segmentation alone does not ensure anatomically consistent measurements. We propose an automated framework that combines deep learning with anatomical reasoning for quantitative cardiac phenotyping. Its anatomical measurement module uses graph geometry, branch identity, and surrounding structures to define vessel-specific pericoronary adipose tissue (PCAT) sampling and a surrogate epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) compartment. The latter also generates training targets without requiring expert pericardium contours. A companion module quantifies coronary calcium on non-contrast CT. Component-wise benchmarking was conducted on five public datasets: ImageCAS, ImageCAS-X, COCA, Visual Lab, and MultiD4CAD. On ImageCAS-X Test-160, lumen segmentation with validation-selected post-processing achieved a Dice score of 0.9133 and a clDice score of 0.940, exceeding the corresponding published CAS-Net point estimates. In the four-split ImageCAS evaluation, the four-member ensemble outperformed the single ResEnc-L model. Analysis of 800 paired scans showed that PCAT attenuation measurements differed between whole-vessel and lumen references. External EAT evaluation showed strong volume correlation and good spatial overlap. Explicit anatomical rules make the resulting measurements interpretable and inspectable. In practice, the framework provides a standardized pipeline for processing cardiac CT cohorts and quantifying coronary calcium, PCAT, and EAT for population-level analysis and comparison across studies. The source code is publicly available at https://github.com/HarryTRAN207/CardiacCT-Research
