# NeuroVLA: A Brain-inspired Embodied Intelligence for Fluid and Fast Reflexive Robotics Control

## Summary

arXiv:2601.14628v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Vision-language-action (VLA) models inherit powerful semantic representations from large language models, but carrying over their design priorities can leave the demands of physical control under-specified. Stable manipulation requires high-frequency proprioceptive feedback and persistent motor state, in addition to instruction understanding. Here we introduce Neuromorphic Vision-Language-Action (NeuroVLA), a brain-inspired architecture that couples a cortical vision-language planner to recurrent cerebellar modulation and a stateful spiking spinal controller. These components translate semantic intent into actions while incorporating high-frequency proprioceptive and force feedback, maintaining short-term motor memory, and enabling local responses to unexpected contact. In simulated and physical manipulation tasks, the cerebellar module reduces mean commanded jerk by 75.6%, and NeuroVLA achieves a 54.8% collision-recovery rate compared with 0.0% for the evaluated VLA baselines. The spinal controller implemented on an FPGA-based neuromorphic processor achieves 2.19 ms inference latency and 0.87 mJ per inference, corresponding to approximately 0.4 W during inference. These results motivate moving beyond inherited language-model design priorities toward embodied architectures that explicitly integrate semantic planning, high-frequency bodily feedback, motor memory, and local correction.
