# Pathology Randomization for Synthetic Priors and Zero-Shot Detection

## Summary

arXiv:2611.21975v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Hospital brain imaging archives hold a diverse array of pathology at sample sizes beyond prospective studies, particularly for underrepresented populations and diseases. To unlock uncurated clinical data and scale analysis, methods are needed to detect abnormalities across contrasts and resolutions. Synthetic data has provided robust, contrast-agnostic models for a variety of human neuroimaging tasks in healthy anatomy, but pathology detection and other tasks in clinical settings still require real patient data, contrast-specific models, or disease-specific generators. We propose PathoDraw, a generative model of brain MRI with pathology. A conditional distribution of lesions given the healthy anatomy is drawn into the label map before contrast-agnostic rendering, and a single network estimates healthy anatomy with exact signed intensity deviation of any input scan. The contrast-agnostic 3D UNet learns the healthy labels under the lesion and the signed deviation directly and identifies pathology via robust tissue statistics and learned features, despite no training on real pathology. We show zero-shot detection on public stroke, tumor, and white matter hyperintensity datasets as well as archived clinical data, spanning $T_1$w, FLAIR and $T_2$w at various resolutions under a recent unsupervised anomaly detection benchmark protocol. Our single model exceeds or performs comparably to contrast-specific anomaly detectors trained on real scans and keeps healthy anatomy intact. The same generator trains networks for super-resolution and for registration in the presence of pathology. Our model, robust across acquisition and disease, enables the use of uncurated, archived data for clinical analysis. Our code is available at https://github.com/agr78/Pathology-Randomizer.
