# Tracing Subliminal Learning Through Pretraining

## Summary

arXiv:2610.30884v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Large language models trained on data generated by other models can sometimes inherit traits unrelated to that data, a phenomenon called subliminal learning. We study both hard label distillation, where students train on numbers generated by a biased teacher, and on-policy soft label distillation, where students generate the data and the teacher's logits provide the learning signal. Using Pythia, PolyPythia, and DataDecide OLMo models, we find that transfer persists in both settings across differences in weight initialization, pretraining data order, pretraining corpus, model size, and architecture. Across pretraining checkpoints, the ability to transmit a preference and the ability to acquire one develop separately: some checkpoints are more effective teachers but weaker students, and vice versa. Consistent with this distinction, cross-model transfer can be strongly asymmetric in magnitude when teacher and student roles are reversed. To investigate what carries the preference signal, we construct synthetic number sequences that match the teacher's generated number frequencies but have no dependence on the prompt or preceding numbers and find that these still induce substantial transfer in the hard-label setting. In top-$K$ soft-label distillation, removing animal tokens from the loss reduces transfer, although not completely in the same-model setting. In our experiments, soft label distillation produces more consistent transfer with less extensive training than hard label distillation. The growing use of on-policy distillation in model post-training makes this a particularly relevant setting for assessing unintended preference transfer.
