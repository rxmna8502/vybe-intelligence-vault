# Analog RF Computing for Integrated Data and AI Flows Over Wireless Networks

## Summary

arXiv:2610.20841v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Many resource-constrained edge devices require artificial intelligence (AI) services but are not powerful enough to store or execute neural networks (NNs). Analog radio frequency (RF) computing addresses this limitation by letting a base station (BS) multicast a waveform encoding NN weights to different clients. Each client then multiplies the received weight-encoded waveform with its locally generated input-encoded waveform through a passive mixer in its RF frontend, thereby realizing the matrix-vector multiplications (MVMs) needed for AI inference with an extremely low power and memory requirement. This can enable telecom operators to provide AI inference services to the edge devices connected to their networks. In practice, however, this multicast AI flow must share the spectrum, power, and spatial resources with other data flows. This paper considers a multi-user multiple-input multiple-output (MU-MIMO) downlink scenario and studies the resulting relative aggregate data rate reduction from integrating the multicast AI flow with the unicast data flows. We formulate a joint beamforming problem for the data and AI flows to maximize the aggregate data rate subject to computing accuracy, client energy, total power, and hardware-related constraints. We develop a weighted minimum mean-square error (WMMSE)-based algorithm to solve the problem. Numerical results based on 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) channels show that the relative aggregate data rate reduction from integrating an AI flow with data flows is below 5% in representative settings. This indicates that telecom operators can use analog RF computing to provide edge AI services while providing data communication services simultaneously.
