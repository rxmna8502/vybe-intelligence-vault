# Function-Preserving Audits of Visual Weight Readers

## Summary

arXiv:2611.05798v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: A neural field can encode the same image with different weights. A reader that recognizes one such representation may therefore rely on parameter coordinates rather than the visual function. We study this distinction through function-preserving transformations, measuring recognition, prediction disagreement, and numerical fidelity together. Across shape, digit, and natural-image fields, high accuracy on the original weights coexists with substantial sensitivity to equivalent representations. On 10,000 official CIFAR10 test origins, one-period hidden-bias shifts change 7.3% of a released HiddenProbe configuration's predictions while barely changing accuracy; three-period shifts change 22.0% and reduce accuracy from 57.8% to 54.4%. Output-only ProbeGen remains unchanged. These effects are smaller than in a separate adapted-reader study, where matched phase and sign controls remove the corresponding sensitivities. Controlled readout studies show why invariance is insufficient: complete canonical hidden features reach 38.7% on MNIST, whereas explicitly composing those activations, outgoing weights, and output bias into field outputs reaches 81.5%. The information is present, but the learned readout does not recover it as effectively. A separate 720-response study of language models reading numerical weights shows why stochastic audits require repeats on both inputs: observed accuracy losses need not yield rejection of response-law equality. Together, these results distinguish invariance, useful visual information, and the evidence required to evaluate stochastic readers.
