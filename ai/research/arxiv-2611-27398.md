# Taking Apart and Putting Together: Tracking Objects Through Physical Transformations

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27398v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: We introduce Transformation Tracking and Understanding (TTU), extending video object tracking to environments where objects undergo physical assemblies and disassemblies. TTU requires continuously tracking objects through transformations, identifying new entities, maintaining a state graph of their relationships, and assigning semantic labels to transformations. While foundation models (e.g., SAM 2/3) maintain tracklets through appearance changes, they cannot update semantic identities as objects physically evolve. Prior work addresses only disassemblies, relying on expensive searches triggered only when tracking fails. We propose Tracking Instances via Proposals and Overlaps (TIPO), a training-free framework that continuously infers transformations via a generate-evaluate-judge pipeline. TIPO generates split and merge proposals from disjoint mask components, scores them against mask-decoder priors using inner and external overlap, and queries an MLLM on the top proposal for visual verification and semantic labeling. To benchmark TTU, we curate Video Instances, States, and Transformation Annotations (VISTA), a dataset including state graphs with 124 assembly and 132 disassembly events across 50 sequences and over 26,000 frames. TIPO achieves state-of-the-art tracking and state-graph accuracy on VISTA, while operating $6\times$ to $15\times$ faster than prior methods. Project page: https://davidegardenal.github.io/tipo-tracking
