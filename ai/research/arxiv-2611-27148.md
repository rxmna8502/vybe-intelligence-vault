# Structure Over Scale: Rethinking Adaptation for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

## Summary

arXiv:2611.27148v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is often framed as a choice between the expressivity of Full Fine-Tuning (FFT) and the efficiency of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). We find that this framing misses an important variable: the geometry of the update. Under a common cold-start GRPO protocol, LoRA exhibits rank collapse: performance remains broadly stable at low rank, then fails abruptly as rank increases. On DeepMath-Hard, for example, accuracy drops from 73.1% at r=64 to 4.7% at r=128. In contrast, Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation (DoRA) and Quantum-Informed Tensor Adaptation (QuanTA) remain stable over a wider tested optimization region and are competitive with FFT while updating less than 0.6% of the model parameters. Spectral analysis reveals a corresponding difference in learned update structure: collapsed LoRA concentrates its update energy into relatively few effective directions, whereas structured adapters produce substantially more distributed updates. These results suggest that nominal rank and parameter count alone are insufficient to characterize adaptation in RLVR - the structure imposed on the reachable update manifold also matters. Finally, across three 8B base models, adapter behavior aligns with both frozen-weight spectral structure and reported pre-training scale, motivating the Model Maturity Hypothesis: the adaptation geometry that works best may depend on properties already encoded during pre-training. We present this as a falsifiable hypothesis rather than a causal conclusion. The code can be found here https://github.com/Shrimpstanot/structure-over-scale
