# Geometry-Preserving Neural Architectures on Manifolds with Boundary

## Summary

arXiv:2602.03082v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: A growing number of neural architectures have been developed to enforce geometric constraints, including projection-based networks, exponential-map updates, and manifold neural ODEs. In this paper, we study several such architectures, with the goal of understanding their approximation-theoretic properties and trade-offs. We distinguish between architectures that enforce constraints at intermediate layers and those that enforce constraints only at the output. For projected neural ODEs on uniformly prox-regular sets, we establish stability estimates and derive universal approximation results on compact smooth manifolds, including manifolds with boundary. In particular, we establish universal approximation of measurable manifold-valued maps in the $L^2$ topology. In contrast, architectures that enforce constraints only at the output can admit uniform approximation guarantees for continuous target maps under suitable geometric assumptions. We further investigate how the choice of constraint-enforcing map, namely metric projection or the Riemannian exponential map, affects these guarantees. While both constructions can be used to preserve constraints at intermediate layers, their use at the output leads to different approximation properties. In particular, exponential-map output architectures can encounter topological obstructions to uniform approximation, motivating approximation guarantees in $L^2$. On the other hand, metric projection-based output architectures can retain uniform approximation guarantees when the constraint set has positive reach. Our study raises the broader question of when the additional complexity of enforcing constraints at intermediate layers, as motivated by neural ODE approaches, and different choices of constraint-enforcing map are justified by the resulting benefits in approximation and learning performance.
