# Regularizing Treatment Leakage for LLM-based Uplift Estimation

## Summary

arXiv:2611.20794v3 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) provide semantic features for uplift estimation, but treatment-revealing text can induce prediction shortcuts that do not transfer to deployment. We propose LLM-based Uplift Leakage Regularization (LULR) to address this risk. With the LLM encoder frozen, LULR penalizes dependence between the causal head's trainable shared representation and treatment while retaining separate outcome heads. The penalty uses a variational approximation to the contrastive log-ratio upper bound (CLUB) of mutual information, targeting treatment dependence rather than generic parameter shrinkage. Controlled proxy experiments provide exploratory evidence of improved ranking scores under injected treatment information relative to no regularization and the tested weight-decay setting. Structured-only benchmarks show modest, dataset-dependent effects. On an industrial pricing randomized controlled trial, mean offline lift@0.3 is 0.258 for LULR, compared with 0.106 for the same-backbone baseline. A separate one-week online A/B test of the complete deployed pricing policy records 5.45% higher sales volume and 1.91% higher gross merchandise value than the control arm. These short-term policy-level gains do not isolate the regularizer's effect.
