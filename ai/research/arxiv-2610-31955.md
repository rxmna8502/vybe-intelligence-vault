# A Mathematical Theory of the Jacobian Lens

## Summary

arXiv:2610.31955v2 Announce Type: replace-cross 
Abstract: Recent work from Anthropic introduced a new interpretability tool, called the Jacobian lens (J-lens, Gurnee et al. 2026). This method identified linear directions in the hidden states of frontier language models that had properties reminiscent of working-memory-like representations. However, it is unclear what mechanistic properties of such features make them detectable by the J-lens. To this end, we introduce a mathematical theory of the J-lens and show that it preferentially identifies coherent features--directions that act like leading, spatially coherent finite-time Lyapunov modes. Small perturbations along these directions maximally amplify across layers, remain roughly constant across token positions, and are consistent across different contexts. While we are agnostic to whether these properties are necessarily associated with a "Global Workspace'', the analytically derived preferences of the J-lens nevertheless explain prior empirical results. We validate our analysis on a Bayes filter and a toy transformer, each optimally predicting next tokens emitted from a Hidden Markov Model (HMM). We show how modulating task parameters that change the coherence of features in the Bayes filter correspondingly and predictively modulates the response of the J-lens applied to both model classes. We also introduce a family of J-lenses built to emphasize different temporal scales of information processing in LLMs, which we call $\omega$-J-lenses.
