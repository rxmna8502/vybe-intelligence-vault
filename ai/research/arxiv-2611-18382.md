# Where Tree Ensembles Decide: Structural Local Explanation with Decision Predicate Graphs

## Summary

arXiv:2611.18382v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Tree ensembles are among the most accurate models for tabular data, yet the computation behind a single prediction is spread over hundreds of decision rules. Feature attributions and counterfactuals explain such predictions through the influence of features or a change of the input, leaving open which of the thresholds applied by the trees a prediction responds to, while structural explanations compress these rules and lose the trees behind them. To address this gap, we propose a local Decision Predicate Graph (local DPG), which gathers the rules executed for a sample into a verifiable record of the computation, with a class support that reproduces every explained prediction. From these rules we select the critical predicate, the executed predicate whose crossing is estimated to move the model most strongly toward a chosen class, which offers the user a concrete threshold and the trees that share it, without an external explainer. Across 16 datasets and four ensemble families, it separates differently predicted neighbours better than random and nearest-threshold predicates. A single crossing recovers the true class in 46-69% of wrong predictions, against 29-50% when the same rule is applied to the feature ranked first by TreeSHAP, a measure close to the selection criterion, and on planted faults it finds the faulty threshold in 46-71% of the resulting errors, against 6-21% at random. Although training-free edits of these predicates did not reduce the error on unseen data, the critical predicate complements attributions and counterfactuals by naming where a prediction can be changed.
