# A single inference process underlies the behavior and neural circuits of working memory

## Summary

arXiv:2611.22054v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Working memory holds past inputs online for future behavior. Despite decades of research, accounts of working memory remain divided across levels of analysis, with long-standing debates on whether its capacity reflects discrete slots or continuous resources, how attention improves recall, and whether its representations rely on persistent neural firing or activity-silent states. Here we show that MemGP, a theory that is specified at the computational, algorithmic, and circuit levels of analysis simultaneously, can explain diverse behavioral and neural phenomena in working memory. At the computational level, MemGP casts working memory as probabilistic inference over a continuous function on feature space, with a variational objective that balances memory strength against a Gaussian-process prior. At the algorithmic level, writing, storing, and reading of memories correspond to approximating this posterior with a finite set of inducing points, the resource that all items share. In simulations, MemGP reproduces load-dependent precision loss, attentional benefits during maintenance, and inter-item attraction and repulsion, in each case revealing multiple complex computational dependencies. At the circuit level, the algorithm analytically (without training) maps onto a recurrent circuit, including its architecture and dynamics. Beyond reproducing neural phenomena such as delay-dependent drift, this analytical map deduces center--surround connectivity, a property long assumed in attractor models, and a structured combination of persistent activity and synaptic weights to hold memories. Thus, a single inference objective not only jointly predicts the behavioral and neural signatures of working memory, but also addresses, across several instances and different levels of analysis, existing competing explanations.
