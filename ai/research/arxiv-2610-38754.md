# Alpha-TCM: Auditable Knowledge Integration for Multimodal TCM Inference

## Summary

arXiv:2610.38754v2 Announce Type: replace 
Abstract: Knowledge-guided multimodal systems need a way to measure how supplied knowledge affects predictions, including when that knowledge is wrong. Alpha-TCM addresses this problem for traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) through an implemented workflow that separates measurements, supplied knowledge, predictions, and reviewer decisions. Observations and contextual knowledge enter separately, and a replaceable prior allows controlled interventions with fixed modality encoders. Bayesian committee accounting removes the reference prior already contained in each encoder before inserting contextual knowledge. A fully simulated confirmation study uses ten paired seeds and four training sizes. Knowledge context reduces prediction error relative to knowledge-free fusion at 600 and 1,200 training cases; equal-information learned context is competitive at 600 and has lower error at larger sizes. Controlled corruption identifies overconfident and displaced priors as the most damaging challenges. Case-level conflict detection remains weak, and training-time prior regularisation shows no consistent benefit. Functional tests verify the connected workflow separately from predictive performance. The simulation demonstrates how Alpha-TCM measures knowledge dependence, separates it from gains due to additional context, and exposes harmful prior assumptions before clinical evaluation.
